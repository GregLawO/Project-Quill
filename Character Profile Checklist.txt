The profile

Answer every question. Short answers are fine, but specific beats general. Where a question asks for a number, give one.

Part A. Who they are

1. Name. Full name, and what people actually call them. Do they have a nickname they dislike?
2. Birth year.
3. Region of origin. The place they grew up, in one or two sentences (city or countryside, rich or poor district, crowded or quiet).
4. First language and any others. Include accent or speech habits.
5. Trade. Their best occupation, and a second-best occupation they could do instead.
6. Money and standing. Do they have savings, debt, or neither? Do they own the tools of their trade? Do other people owe them?
7. Appearance. Two or three details that someone would notice across a room. Avoid generic words like "handsome" and "striking."

Part B. What drives them

8. Small want. One thing they want soon, concretely (a signature, a repaired roof, a night's sleep).
9. Large want. One thing they want in the long run, even if they would not say it aloud.
10. Fear. What they will not risk, and why.
11. Wound. One thing that happened to them that still shapes how they act today.
12. Pride. One thing they are good at, and how they act when someone tells them they are wrong about it.

Part C. How they see the world

13. Habit of attention. What they notice first when they walk into a room. It must be specific (not "everything," not "people"). Examples: the exits, the shoes, the prices, who is not speaking, what is broken. Pick one, and pick something you can imagine in a sentence of prose.
14. What they misread. One thing they consistently get wrong about other people or the world.
15. View of the age. The cures, the ships, the machines: in one or two sentences, do they trust the new world, fear it, profit from it, or resent it? Why?
16. Humor. Do they joke? When, and about what? If they never do, say so.

Part D. How they behave

17. Speech. Write three sample lines of dialogue in different moods (calm, frustrated, and one when they are lying or avoiding something). Name one verbal habit (a phrase they repeat, a way they trail off, how they address strangers).
18. Body tell. One physical detail that shows what they feel without anyone naming the feeling (a hand that smooths a sleeve, a jaw that works, a habit of counting stairs). The Author uses it in tense scenes.
19. Stress response. When things go wrong, do they freeze, bargain, deflect, take charge, or run? Give a short example from their past.
20. Honesty. On a scale of 1 to 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie), where are they, and what do they lie about, if anything?
21. Authority. How do they treat people with power over them, and people they have power over?

Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. Who they protect. Up to three people or groups, in order.
23. Who they distrust, and why.
24. Who or what they owe. A debt of money, favor, or gratitude.
25. Loyalty under pressure. If two of their loyalties conflict, which one do they choose? Give an example of a choice they have already made, and say what it cost them.
26. Line and price. One thing they will never do, and what situation could make them do it anyway.
27. Secret. One thing they hide from nearly everyone. It does not have to be dark. It must matter to them.

Part F. How they change

28. Adaptability. Name one thing about this person that would still be true if their life went completely differently from what you expect.
29. What could break them. What would have to happen for them to change at their core?
30. What could redeem them. What small kindness or small act of courage is in them, even on their worst day?

### Part G. Three rules for the Author

Write three "if, then" rules that describe your character in any situation. They must work in any scene, so they must not depend on a job or place. Format: If [situation\], then they [action].

- Weak: If a customer is rude, then they stay polite. (Depends on a job.)
- Strong: If someone they trust is watching them get cornered, then they get quiet and calm instead of loud.

31. Rule 1: If ___, then they ___.
32. Rule 2: If ___, then they ___.
33. Rule 3: If ___, then they ___.

Part H. Optional

34. One object they carry or keep, and why.
35. One memory they return to when they cannot sleep.
