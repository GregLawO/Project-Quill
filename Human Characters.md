# Human_Characters.md

**Purpose.** The roster for the whole cast. It holds what no single profile can: story-wide rulings, the age and role-fit tables, relationships and secrets between characters, the POV roster, and full entries for every character who has no profile of their own. Characters with a profile (Kenji, Hannah, Brooks, Manswell, Tom, Ruth, Danny, Vance, Ashby, Byron, Marchetti, Whitcombe) get a pointer line here, plus any fact the published and drafted chapters have added that the profile lacks. The profile governs voice, habits, appearance, and personal facts. Nothing a profile already says is repeated in this file.

**Scope of this copy.** This file integrates the earlier drop-in updates with the Act I Outline (3 November 2026 revision) and the drafted A1 and A2.1 to A2.10. The original full file is not in the project, so its other rulings (2, 4, 6 to 10, 12 to 14, 16, 18, 22 to 27), the entries for characters not named here (Harper, Anderson, Hackett, Grissom, Zaeed, Udina, and the rest), Section 8A's recurring OCs, and the full Section 10 are not reproduced. Those stay in the original and should be merged in. Decisions that the author left to me are marked (decision).

## 1. Rulings

**1. The Protagonist.** His name is **Kenji Himura**, of Japanese descent. His body is thirty in 2050 (born 2020). He marries Hannah in September 2058.

**3. Alexandra's birth.** In 2059, five months pregnant, Hannah survives a bomb at the Galveston clinic complex, but the child she carries is lost. The attack was carried out for the conspiracy (ruling 28), though it was not the conspiracy's first move, since its first moves date from 2055. Its aim was to cut Kenji's network off at its central clinic, and the child's death was a cost its planners accepted. No one in the world identifies the attackers, and no one claims it. Danny Shepard, Hannah's adoptive brother, dies outside the building in the same blast. A delivery van that should have come never arrived, a different van took its place, and the gate guard had been bought. Danny was on overwatch in the car park, outside Hannah's guard roster, saw the van riding low, and ran at it. He got the driver's door open before it went off, short of the east wing. In the corridor afterward her husband says: "Never again. No child. Not until no one can reach you." Hannah answers: "Not until then." The oath, the wait, and the 2153 conception stand as written.

**5. The resistance and the conspiracy.** Canon's Cerberus does not exist. Harper's black cell is the **Third Hand**, inside the **Charter Front**. The author-side label "Cerberus" belongs to a separate organization, the conspiracy of ruling 28, which has no link to Harper or the Front.

**11. Who knows what.** Nobody in the story is ever told that Kenji is a transmigrator, and nobody learns where his foresight comes from. The reader learns it from his POV (Overview.md). Hannah learns the enemy's name in 2170 (age 139). Brooks and Alexandra learn it later, as set in the Overview. Until then Kenji gives only his hypothetical: what if what killed the aliens on Mars, or the makers of the alien FTL artifacts, came back hostile? Vance knows he traded his memories for treatment and that the droids' combat routines came from them. He is never told how Kenji knows what he knows, and he does not know about the Reapers. Nobody else is told where the droids' fighting style comes from.

**15. The framing of 2125.** The public record says the delegates were killed by the Russian Federation. No in-universe document contains evidence of the truth. The truth, which POVs may show:
- A few UNIN delegates were verified members of the conspiracy, working with channels inside the Russian Federation. They were partly responsible for the 2059 bombing and the 2065 withdrawals. Brooks found this and reported it. Kenji decided on the operation, and Brooks carried it out. His motive was revenge.
- **The count (decision).** The delegation held fifteen delegates. Fourteen died in the attack. Four of the fourteen were the verified conspirators. The other ten were innocent and died as cover. The fifteenth was absent that day and lived. Kenji carries the ten as a cost he chose.
- Harper's file is right that the Russian Federation was framed for the killing. It cannot see that Russia did conspire against Kenji, and it cannot say by whom. He proves nothing.
- The UNIN does not annul the charters. It decries its helplessness to win justice for its slain, and Kenji takes up the burden of justice for all humankind.

**17. The Protagonist's POV.** Kenji carries POV from A2 on, **written in the first person** (author ruling, 3 November 2026). He carries 7 of A2's 20 micro-chapters (A2.1, A2.4, A2.6, A2.9, A2.12, A2.13, A2.15), none of A3's 10, and 1 of A4's 10. In every other chapter he carries at most 3 or 4 per 10 micro-chapters. No Kenji POV is dated after 2064 until B1.2, where the realization lands. A1 stays as published, with him unnamed and without POV. From the proclamation in 2125 he is also the public Emperor, seen by every POV.

**19. The early pool.** Before 2072 the characters old enough to carry POV are Kenji, Hannah, Brooks, Manswell, Tom and Ruth Shepard, Vance (ruling 30), Teodor Ashby (b. 2030, locked, Author-built), and the three main-cast recruits of ruling 33 (Marchetti, Byron Pyrrus, and Eleanor Whitcombe). The found-family servants and network operatives carry no POV. In chapters A to C, up to two of five micro-chapters per chapter are generic OC slots. Movement 1 (A1 to A3) is exempt from the 20 percent guide, because its OC voices carry the technology showcase (9 of 35). From Movement 2 on, generic OC voices stay under 20 percent (7 of 50). In D to F at most three of the five added slots may be OC, and G keeps one.

**20. Reveal discipline.** POVs may show the truth of the 2065 withdrawals, the 2125 killing, and the conspiracy where the POV character could know it. No in-universe document may contain it, and the conspiracy never names itself on the page before Kenji's network discovers it.

**21. Democracy against empire.** The argument is seen through Hannah's, Brooks's, and Kenji's POVs.

**28. The conspiracy.** Cerberus is an organization that acts against Kenji from 2055, when its first buyer approaches a client's physician. Its method is shadow control of the world's most advanced technologies, his above all, through cutouts, firms, regulators, and licensors. It is called "Cerberus" or "they," never "it." Its moves are the physician's offer (Nov 2055), the theft of five Level 2 Med-Pods (Jun 2057), which it cannot reproduce, three offers to buy Oyama's patents (Jan, Apr, and Oct 2058), the Galveston raid (Nov 2058), the 2059 bomb, and the forged letter that closes Galveston (Oct 2061). The network learns the name at the third offer (see ruling 34). After these the network knows it as one organization but not who leads it, and Brooks is recruited in 2062 to find out. The four delegates of 2125 were partly responsible for the 2059 bomb and the 2065 withdrawals only, so it is larger than they were. It survives 2125. It is author-side in the chapters, and its payoff depends on how the story unfolds. The 2056 shakedown at Galveston is not Cerberus: it is a hospital chain that lost patients to the charity floors.

**29. Patron seats.** Ten patron-built OC seats exist (Overview.md). Their roles are hidden from patrons and kept in an author-only file, never here. Patrons cannot influence Kenji, Hannah, Tom, Ruth, Brooks, or any canon or OC character the author has not opened.

**30. Dominic Vance.** Gunnery Sergeant Dominic Vance, USMC, retired (name and rank are placeholders), is a locked recurring OC and member of the main cast. Facts his profile lacks:
- In Act I he carries POV in A2.7 (2051 to spring 2059) and A3.1 (2062) only, and his A3.1 must match every gesture of the published A1.5.
- Kenji uses the copy of his memories to create and hone the security droids' combat abilities, and the droids' routines are the only place it lives. This supersedes "knowledge upload never spreads" for this case only.
- He is the first recruit and first member of the secret network (summer 2051) and its first leader. He brings in Ashby (autumn 2051), expands the team twice before Byron is recruited (July 2052), and walks with Kenji until the L5 guards are finished (2055). He scouts Brooks, recruits her, and brings her to Kenji. When Brooks takes over the network he becomes Kenji's personal guard, and later Legion Master of the Custodian Legion.

**31. Security and operatives (author ruling, revised 3 November 2026).** The network begins in summer 2051 with Vance, then Ashby, who guard Kenji. Vance's fighters wear Level 1 powered-suit frames, and the date of Level 2 suits is open. From autumn 2052 the private sites are guarded by Level 2 security droids built by Oyama, run by Level 2 Dumb AI on Kenji's data crystals, and upgraded to Level 3 after the June 2057 theft (decision). Two Level 5 droids are with Kenji at all times from 2055. They look and move like men, and Hannah takes them for men until 2060 (A2.12). Operatives carry no POV, apart from Vance, who is main cast and not an operative in this sense.

**32. Med-Pod tiers and longevity (author ruling).** Only Level 2 Med-Pods and better can rejuvenate and extend longevity. The public Primitive grade cures and mends only. Anyone in this file who lives past a natural span was treated at Level 2 or better. Tom and Ruth Shepard live to 116 because Kenji gives them Level 2 treatment as found family, and not because of the public clinics (decision). Tom is treated in the hidden wing in 2053, because his adopted son Danny asked Kenji to save him. Ruth takes the same treatment after she meets Kenji, and refuses the upload (decision). Where older text says public-grade for a long-lived character, it is superseded by this ruling. Every Level 2 treatment carries the silence directive (ruling 40) except the found family's.

**33. The recruits who become main cast (author ruling, 3 November 2026).** Byron Pyrrus (owner of Oyama Industries, a Mass Effect canon corporation), Helen Marchetti (a Houston lawyer, Tallis Bay's trustee), and Eleanor Whitcombe (a senator who leads the organization's political factions) are main cast. Each later becomes one of the Imperium's top administrators (titles open). Kenji recruits Byron in July 2052, which is why Whitcombe is not the first elite buyer. Names, ages, and details are placeholders unless stated. Each has a profile and a POV chapter in A2 (A2.3, A2.2, A2.5), and Whitcombe has a second in A4.3.

**34. How Cerberus is named (author ruling, 3 November 2026).** The network does not learn the name from money trails or documents. It learns it at the third attempt to buy Oyama's technology patents (October 2058), when the other side's lawyer tries to frighten Marchetti with the threat of violence from "Cerberus." Marchetti says nothing at the table, drives to the warehouse that night, and tells Vance. He writes the word in his green book. The raiders of November 2058 say nothing.

**35. Oyama Industries and the robots (author ruling, 3 November 2026).** Oyama builds the Primitive Med-Pods after Byron is recruited (Kenji builds no Primitive pod). It also sells Level 1 to Level 3 robots, Level 1 powered suits, Tokamak reactors, Level 1 anti-gravity, and Gundarium Alpha once orbital infrastructure is complete. Byron is also the first target of Cerberus. Oyama puts its name on every plate, with a blank line where the designer should be.

**36. The terms of service (as drafted in A2.2 and A2.3).** Every recruit serves seven years. Treatment continues for as long as they serve. Renewal needs the person's own signature each time, and nobody can renew on their behalf, Kenji included, because a term someone else can extend is a lease and not a choice. The foundation Tallis Bay carries every term and every treatment, and Marchetti is its trustee, so she alone can stop Kenji. If Tallis Bay ever stops paying, every design it owns becomes Oyama's to build and sell, and Marchetti drafted that clause to protect Byron's workers.

**37. Disclosure and the two layers of conditioning (decision, from A2.2, A2.7, and the outline).** Recruits are told about the secrecy directive before they sign, and it outlasts the seven years. The first layer, a secrecy directive carried by the upload, goes to the first recruits from 2051. The second layer, which makes selling Kenji out something a person cannot do, goes to every member of the cadre two weeks after Danny's funeral in spring 2059. Vance asks to be left out of it, and Kenji agrees. Primaris adds a further layer later (see the Legions file). Hannah, Tom, and Ruth are the found family and are not conditioned. Tom takes the training upload but refuses the directive, and Ruth refuses the upload. The silence directive on Level 2 treatment is a separate matter (ruling 40).

**38. What Hannah knows about Danny (decision, from the outline and A2.6 to A2.12).** Hannah knows her adoptive brother Danny is alive and believes he works for a security contractor in Houston doing gates and fences. Tom and Ruth learn in 2053 that he works for a company Kenji runs, and they keep it from her at Kenji's request. Hannah learns the truth about Danny and about the two guards in spring 2060 (A2.12), after the bomb.

**39. Kenji's promise to Hannah (author ruling, from A2.11 and the outline).** In December 2057 he tells her: "I will never say a false thing to you." From then on he answers with true sentences that say nothing, as in B2.9 ("friends' money"). He says "I can't answer that" when he will not answer. Later chapters must stay true but empty.

**40. The silence directive on Level 2 treatment (decision, 5 November 2026).** Every Level 2 rejuvenation and longevity treatment carries a silence directive that stops the patient from divulging who, how, where, when, and why (Protagonist and Timeline, item 2F). It covers the elites from April 2055 and anyone else treated outside the recruit and found-family tracks. It is separate from the recruits' upload layers (ruling 37) and does not make betrayal unthinkable. Patients are told the terms before treatment. Recruits and managers are covered by their own conditioning. The found family is treated without it. Public Primitive patients are only asked for discretion. A treated patient may say they were treated and nothing more of the five details, so Cerberus's proof from 2055 is what bodies show and never what patients say.
