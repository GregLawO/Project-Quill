# Excerpt Bank

**Labels (author ruling).** Parts are the seven groups, A to G. Chapters are A1, A2, A3, and so on (32 in all). Micro-chapters are the single-POV pieces inside a chapter, coded A1.1, A1.2, and so on. Each chapter opens with its excerpt, the quoted passage that is its epigraph and base.

**The excerpts (author ruling).** The excerpt text stays word for word. From A2 on it is read as the account of an uninformed independent scholar (author ruling, revised 1 November 2026). A1 stands as published. The micro-chapters carry what the scholar cannot see, so every excerpt can be incomplete or wrong. The excerpts carry no first-person voice and no sign of who wrote them.

**Status of the casts (revised 1 November 2026).** Only A1 is published and canon, at 5 micro-chapters. A2 (20, four batches of 5) and A3 (10, two batches) go live on 2 November 2026 and were reopened and revised, so they are not canon yet. A4 to B4 (Movement 2) are cast at 10 micro-chapters each, released 5 at a time. Micro-chapters are ordered by date inside each chapter, with two deliberate exceptions: A3.1 (2062) leads A3 as a step back to A1.5, and A2.4 is a ledger from 2052 to 2061 ordered by its start date. C1 to G5 still carry their original casts of 5 (D to F carry 7 to 9), apart from C6, which stays at 5. The others will be extended to 10 each when drafted. Act I as cast is 120 micro-chapters. ## Part A: Gifts Without a Giver
*The cures, the first ships, the new machines, and the unanswered question of where they came from.*

### A1. Those Who Walked Out Whole

The clinics were built around a single apparatus, the Med-Pod. Patients entered it ill or broken and, by their own accounts, left restored. The accounts do not gush. They are written in a stunned, flat register, like those of survivors of an accident who have not yet decided whether to believe they are alive. Patients crossed borders to reach the clinics. Families waited to see a cure work on a neighbor before trusting it with their own. By the 2060s the clinics stood in all the larger economies, though standing everywhere is a different matter from being accepted.

*Published. Cast: A1.1 Hannah (22), 2053; A1.2 OC (exposition), 2058; A1.3 OC (outlook), 2059; A1.4 Hannah (28), 2059; A1.5 Brooks (24), 2062.*

### A2. The Centuries That Became Days

Then, in the 2060s, ships appeared that could cross to another star in days. The papers of the time give their pace as about a light year a day. The chronicles show crowds at the first departures, and governments scrambling to understand what had been placed in their hands.

### A4. The Inventor Nobody Could Name

## Part D: A Thousand Flags

### D1. As Far as the Ships Could Safely Go

The world scattered outward on its own. Governments and companies each sent settlers as far as their own ships could safely take them. ## Part F: The Reckoning
*The reckoning after the war, the proclamation recalled, the relay quarantine, and the closed relays.*

### F1. One Law, One Reckoning

The terms had been stated on the first day of the war and never changed: a single body of law under the Imperium of Sol, and for every polity a choice between conceding and facing justice, or being crushed. There was no amnesty. Aid for rebuilding went only to regions that had stood aside from the war. Some polities conceded early, believing in the cause. Many more refused and were crushed. In 2150, when the Alliance conceded, each surviving polity, whether it had bent or been broken, was dissolved into a province under Imperial law and policed by the Inquisition, and veterans from every side were screened.
