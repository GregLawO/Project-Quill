# Specific Rules (Compiled)

One place for every rule that governs how a chapter is written. It gathers the rules from Overview.md (Writing Rules), the Elastic Pace System (Revised), the Drafting Checklist (Project Version), Act_I_Outline.md (Sections 4 and 5), Human_Characters.md, Excerpt_Bank.md (casting rules), the character profiles, and the Revision Log.

**Scope.** Rules that apply to every chapter live in General Writing Rules.md and are not repeated here. This file holds what is specific to this story: its chapters, characters, dates, tiers, and secrets. Style rules (word ranges, gears, endings, voice sheet, checklists) live in the Writing Style section of General Writing Rules.md. Kenji's moved rulings are marked *(moved from General)*. Section numbers are unchanged so older references still work.

**Status labels.** An entry the author states, or approves with "Auto approved," is fact. (inference) means a consequence drawn from settled facts. (open) means unsettled. (author ruling) means the author stated it and it supersedes earlier text. (decision) means my choice, open to overrule.

---

## 1. Process and format

- **Order.** The story is written chapter by chapter, starting with the Prologue. It is written until it is finished. There is no fixed word count.
- **Labels.** Parts are A to G. Chapters are A1, A2, and so on (32). Micro-chapters are the single-POV pieces inside a chapter, coded A1.1, A1.2, and so on. "Excerpt" means only the quoted epigraph that opens a chapter.
- **Published canon.** Only A1 is published. It is never rewritten. A2 and A3 were reopened and revised before their 2 November 2026 release, so they are not canon yet. The first release is the first batch of A2 (A2.1 to A2.5).
- **Chapter sizes.** A1 has 5 micro-chapters. A2 has 20, released as four batches of 5. A3 has 10, released as two batches. From A4 on every chapter has 10, released 5 at a time. C6 stays at 5. C1 to C5 and C7 are extended from 5 to 10 when drafted.
- **Date-order exceptions.** Two deliberate exceptions: A3.1 (2062) leads A3 as a step back to A1.5, and A2.4 is told as a ledger from 2052 to 2061 and is ordered by its start date.
- **Micro-chapter length (A2b).** A2b chapters run about 850 to 900 words and may hold two or three dated scenes in one POV.
- **Chapter excerpt header format (author ruling).** Each excerpt comes from Excerpt_Bank.md and sits at the very top of the draft, above any title or byline:
  1. The excerpt's title only, as a heading, with no code and no "Excerpt" label.
  2. The excerpt text in italics inside quotation marks.
  3. An attribution line: a dash, then *The Road to the Stars*. No author named.
- **Saving a draft.** When a micro-chapter is added to a chapter that already has a draft, write the whole chapter file (existing micro-chapters unchanged, the new one appended). Put drafts in `/mnt/user-data/outputs/`, call `present_files`, and tell the author the draft is not yet in the Project.
- **Revision commentary** does not belong in the outline or other story files. The Revision Log holds the history. New entries go into the real Revision Log, not separate log files.

## 2. The excerpts

- **An uninformed account (author ruling).** From A2 on, read each excerpt as the account of an independent scholar who cannot see what the micro-chapters show. The micro-chapters may complete or contradict it. A1 stands as published.
- The excerpts carry no first-person voice and no sign of who wrote them.

## 10. Kenji's POV rules

- **No Reaper thoughts and no past-fiction thoughts before 2065 (moved from General).** Kenji does not think that the world is from a story he once read, and he does not think about the Reapers, until 2065.
- **No Kenji chapter is set in 2065 or later until B1.2 (moved from General).** B1.2 is where he realizes where he is. A later Kenji chapter would show his thoughts and give that away early.
- **At most three sentences of past life per chapter (moved from General).** They come only through small habits, such as finishing every meal.
- **Nobody, in a chapter or a document, says how Kenji knows what he knows (moved from General).** No character is ever told.
- **From A2 on.** Kenji carries POV. A1 stays as published: unnamed, no POV.
- **Count.** He carries 8 of A2's 20 (A2.1, A2.2, A2.3, A2.5, A2.7, A2.10, A2.12, A2.15), none of A3's 10, and 1 of A4's 10. In every other chapter he carries at most 3 or 4 per 10. *(open: the cap of 7 in older files is lifted to 8 by the third revision and still needs the author's ruling.)*
- **Before 2065.** The knowledge arrives in his hands before his head. His unease is a cautious man's unease. He may suspect he has transmigrated (revealed at A2.1).
- **From 2065.** He may name his dread once per chapter at most. His past-life habits include flinching at being wanted.
- **Speech.** Short declaratives, few metaphors, and workshop and tool images when he does use one. He asks "What do you need?" more often than he gives orders. Dry humor is rare.
- **Fate habit.** He never says "it can't get worse." He notes what arrives early or late and does not call it luck.
- **Fast Gear tell.** He squares objects on a desk and washes his hands. His own chapters never name shaking hands.
- **Fate (author ruling).** He thinks and feels as if the universe is working against him. The text mostly allows chance or human causes to explain what happens. Fate costs him materials, money, time, people, and trust, never the end state.
- **Kenji and Hannah (A2b).** Every A2b micro-chapter has at least one scene where the two talk, and something between them is different by the end of it. The change shows in what they say and do, not in what an object means. Kenji tells Hannah no false thing (A2.9 promise): later chapters such as B2.9 ("friends' money") must stay true but empty.

## 11. The marine (Vance)

- Gunnery Sergeant Dominic Vance, USMC, retired.
- Locked recurring OC and main cast. In Act I he carries POV in **A2.4** (told as a dated ledger, 2052 to 2061) and **A3.1** (2062) only.
- **A3.1 must match every gesture of the published A1.5:** gray coat in July, the quarter-turned chair, the four-second pause, the hidden sidearm.
- He reads ground and counts nothing, so he does not clash with Brooks.
- He is not conditioned in his early years and stays by choice. He receives conditioning with the Primaris Process when he joins the guard (decision).
- Only Kenji and Vance know his memories became the droids' fighting style. No other character is told.

## 12. Secrecy and reveal discipline

- **Reaper secrecy.** Only Kenji, Hannah, Brooks, and Alexandra (from her twenty-first birthday) know of the Reapers, and they learn it from him much later. No chapter, document, or character outside the inner circle may refer to an unnamed enemy that the drills are for. Kenji's stated justification is a hypothetical: what if what killed the aliens on Mars, or the makers of the alien FTL artifacts, came back hostile?
- **The conspiracy** begins in 2051 with the release of his technology. Four probes before 2065 read to every POV as ordinary greed: the break-in (A2.3), the stolen Primitive Med-Pod (A2.4), the clinic shakedown (A2.6), the "partnership" offer (A4.3). No POV links them before 2065, Brooks logs them as unrelated files, and no document connects them. No chapter names the conspiracy before Kenji's network discovers it.
- **In-universe documents** never contain the conspiracy, the true cause of the 2065 withdrawals, Kenji's role in the Charon flare, or the truth of the 2125 killing, which appears in documents only as the act of the Russian Federation. POVs may show the truth where they could know it. A document may note the timing of the withdrawals but not name a cause.
- **Kenji's name** may appear in documents for minor matters only.
- **The 2065 withdrawals.** Kenji did not order the squeeze and does not know who did. He proves nothing.
- **Hannah's list of questions she chose not to ask** stays secret. It is shown only inside her head, and no character sees it.
- **No human meets an alien, and the Citadel appears nowhere.**
- **The Citadel** has no data on relays it never used and cannot know they exist. Nothing it could not have seen may be written as something it saw.

## 13. Network, robots, and technology

- **Robots first.** From 2052 the private sites are guarded by Level 2 robot sentries under Level 1 Dumb AI task controllers. No human operative exists before the network, and none appears in a chapter dated earlier.
- **When Kenji uses his technology (author ruling).** He keeps his advanced technology and robots from the public until a time he chooses. But he never refuses to use them when someone he holds dear is in danger, and he will use them whenever the use can be disguised or hidden. Keeping a technology secret never outranks keeping his people safe. When he does use it, he makes it look ordinary, as he did with the anonymous tip and the "intoxicated" report in A2.3.
- **The network** is founded at the end of A2.3 (2052) with Vance as first member. It exists before Kenji meets the Shepards, grows in A2.4, and Galveston opens in A2.5.
- **Operatives carry no POV**, apart from Vance.
- **Tier examples for robots (author ruling):**
  - **Primitive (Level 1):** immobile, or it needs a human controller.
  - **Level 2:** acts on its own, but moves clankily and loudly. The most advanced AI it can support is Level 2.
  - **Level 3:** moves more smoothly, but is still very loud. The most advanced AI it can support is Level 3.
  - **Level 4 and above:** follow the same pattern, with the exact movement and noise still to be set (open).
- **Tiers.** Kenji starts the tier system in A2.2: a Primitive grade for what may leave the room, a higher level for what stays. The Primitive grade must always be one the world could have invented unaided. Public and private levels by 2060 are in Act_I_Outline.md, Section 2.
- **Warp.** Flights in A2.13 to A2.15 are unmanned and private. The first crewed crossing beyond the Solar System is A2.17 (2064).
- **Hannah's firm (decision).** She leaves complex security after the 2059 bomb and founds a private firm in 2060.
- **Unconfirmed tier tables.** The Med-Pod and Anti-Gravity levels (public L1, private L2) are decisions pending the author's definitions.

## 14. Casting rules

- **POV age.** A file character carries POV only from age 8, only while alive, and only for what they could see or know.
- **Early pool (before 2072).** Kenji, Hannah, Brooks, Manswell, Tom, Ruth, Vance, and Ashby (b. 2030, locked, Author-built; first appears A2.14, 2063). Found-family servants and network operatives carry no POV.
- **A2b POVs.** Hannah: A2.6, A2.8, A2.9. Kenji: A2.7, A2.10. Tom, Ruth, the surgeon, and the rigger appear in scenes and carry no POV.
- **A3** has no Kenji POV, because it is dated after the 2065 realization apart from A3.1.
- **Generic OC slots.** Movement 1 is exempt from the 20 percent guide, because its OC voices carry the technology showcase. Movement 2 stays under 20 percent (8 of 50). In C, up to two of five are OC. In D to F, at most three of the five added slots. G keeps one.
- **Patron seats.** Ten patron-built OC seats exist. Their roles are kept in an author-only file, never in the bible. Patrons cannot influence Kenji, Hannah, Tom, Ruth, Brooks, Vance, or any canon or OC character the author has not opened.
- **Argument over empire.** Hannah argues against empire and Brooks presses the question (D1.4, E1.1, F2.1, F2.2). Kenji's answer: democracy has had its chance and has lasted poorly beside Rome, whose legacies are far better.
- **The 2125 count.** Fifteen delegates. Fourteen died, four of them verified conspirators, ten innocent. The fifteenth was absent.

## 15. Character rules (from the profiles)

- Each character keeps a verbal habit: Tom "Show me," Hannah "Walk me through it," Ruth "And who is looking after you?", Brooks "For the record," Vance "Walk the ground," Ashby "Listen to that," Manswell "Let me put a number on that," Kenji "What do you need?"
- **Honesty scale (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie).** Tom 4, Ashby 4, Hannah 4, Ruth 4, Vance 4, Kenji 4, Manswell 3, Brooks 2.

### Each character's "Avoid" list
- **Tom.** Only comic relief or only the wise old man. Working out the secrets himself. Long speeches.
- **Ashby.** Only the nerd or only the damaged veteran. Long technical explanations of the drive. Jokes about the dead squad.
- **Manswell.** Only a fool or only a villain. Simple gratitude to his rescuer. Sudden bravery not built up earlier.
- **Hannah.** Only the wronged wife or grieving mother. Speeches. Convenient forgiveness, or grief that ends because the plot needs it to.
- **Vance.** Only muscle or a tough-guy cliché. Explaining the setting. Being smarter than everyone about money or law.
- **Kenji.** Smug-genius scenes. Enjoying being thanked. Anyone being told where his knowledge came from. Past-life references over three sentences per chapter.
- **Ruth.** Only a worried mother or only a comfort. Being a victim. Working out secrets by herself. Long speeches.
- **Brooks.** Villain monologues. Cruelty for its own sake. Enjoying the count of the dead. Sudden warmth the story has not earned.

### Rules for the Author (if-then, by character)
- **Tom.** Unsure something is safe: takes it apart on paper first and lets no one rely on it. Someone he trusts hides something: watches their hands and says nothing until sure. Someone he loves is in danger: takes the physical job nearest them.
- **Ashby.** A machine runs perfectly and quietly: distrusts it and checks harder. He hears something wrong: reports it at once, in writing. Told not to worry: says "all right" and keeps listening.
- **Manswell.** Offered money: asks who and what it costs, then says yes anyway if he has no choice. Challenged in public: stays friendly and answers with a specific number or offer. A promise to someone who depends on him: keeps it.
- **Hannah.** Given a partial answer by someone she loves: goes quiet and does not ask for the rest. Someone in her care is in danger: moves toward it. Disagrees with an order: says so once, plainly, then carries it out.
- **Vance.** Enters a room: finds the exits and best position first. Someone in his care is in danger: puts himself between. Told to guard something without being told the threat: asks to walk the ground first.
- **Kenji.** Someone near him is in trouble: asks what they need before what happened. Afraid: makes a list and starts item one. Thanked, wanted, or told he is loved: changes the subject to a task and straightens something on the desk.
- **Ruth.** Someone is with a child: judges them by it. Someone is hurting: gives them food or drink before any question. A decision would harm a child in her care: says no, politely and firmly, in front of the people who want a yes.
- **Brooks.** Something does not add up: keeps working long past anyone else, tells no one until she has the full picture. Given an order she disagrees with: states her objection once, completely, then carries it out. Someone is kind and wants nothing: goes quiet and looks for what they want.

### Body tells
Tom turns a tool over. Ashby tilts his head and touches the stethoscope. Manswell sets his watch face down. Hannah presses her thumb along the scar. Vance takes a slow breath, and in bad moments rests a hand on the sidearm. Kenji squares objects and washes his hands. Ruth straightens a napkin or collar. Brooks's thumb walks across her fingertips.

## 17. Draft checklist

**Format**
- [ ] File opens with the excerpt header (title, italic quoted text, attribution)
- [ ] Byline and place line match the previous chapter's format
- [ ] One POV only
- [ ] Word count in range

**Style**
- [ ] Slow Gear paragraphs are 3 to 4 sentences, never more than 4, sentences mostly 15 to 25 words
- [ ] Fast Gear paragraphs are one or two lines, sentences of 2 to 7 words, sparing adjectives and adverbs
- [ ] Gear shift: trigger, paragraph break, thought purge, one body detail that stays
- [ ] Ending type matches the outline
- [ ] "But" used where the second clause cuts against expectation
- [ ] Every sentence can be read once and understood
- [ ] Plain, everyday language

**Canon**
- [ ] Consistent with the outline, character profile, and these rules
- [ ] Emotion carried as the style requires (head and noticing in the Slow Gear, body in the Fast Gear)
- [ ] Kenji: no Reaper or past-fiction thoughts before 2065, at most three sentences of past life per chapter, no Kenji chapter set in 2065 or later before B1.2
- [ ] No in-universe document or character reveals how Kenji knows what he knows
