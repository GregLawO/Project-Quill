# Hannah Shepard: Character Profile

Anything I invented to fill a gap is marked **(decision)** and is yours to overrule. The profile is the base for how Hannah acts in every scene.

## Part A. Who she is

1. **Name.** Hannah Shepard. Her family and her husband call her "Hannah." Her father called her "Darling" and she answered to it only from him. She dislikes "Empress," the title she carries from 2125, because it describes where she stands and not what she does. **(decision)**
2. **Birth year.** 2031. She is twenty-two when her father is cured and when she refuses the knowledge upload. Her age matters because she outlives nearly everyone she grows up with. She is a hundred and forty-four in 2175, and she still looks and moves like a woman in her prime.
3. **Region of origin.** Galveston, Texas, a shipyard district of cranes, hurricane shutters, and families who have worked the same docks for generations. Everyone in her street knew everyone else's wages and everyone else's illnesses.
4. **Languages.** English with a Gulf Coast Texas accent that flattens over the decades and comes back when she is tired or angry. She picked up working Spanish from the yard crews as a girl and uses it to calm frightened people. **(decision)**
5. **Trade.** Best: commander of escorts and convoys, first with her own private security firm and later in fleet command. Second-best: security chief for a facility, the job she held at the Galveston clinic complex, where she checked other people's work for the thing that would fail. She began as a Navy officer in 2053 at age 22, left the Navy around 2056, and founded her firm in 2060, after the bomb. **(decision)**
6. **Money and standing.** As a young woman she has debt from her father's illness and no savings. Later money stops mattering to her, because everything she touches is paid for by someone else. She owns very little, mostly her father's toolbox. A good number of people owe her their lives, and she never mentions it.
7. **Appearance.**
   - A pale burn scar runs the length of her left forearm, from the 2059 blast. She never covers it.
   - Her hair is cut short and practical, and her uniform is always squared and clean.
   - She stands with her weight on both feet, never leaning, and her hands are always busy with something while she listens.

## Part B. What drives her

8. **Small want.** A straight answer from someone. On any given day, to get through it without discovering another missing record, person, or explanation.
9. **Large want.** To raise a child who lives, and to be told the whole truth by the man she married. She would trade a great deal of the second for the first.
10. **Fear.** Losing a child again and being used without knowing it. She will not risk the people under her command on a plan she has not been allowed to understand.
11. **Wound.** The 2059 bomb at the Galveston clinic complex where she ran security. She was five months pregnant. She got the night staff out first and lost the child. Later she learns there is a second wound underneath the first: someone knew more than they told her.
12. **Pride.** Keeping people alive who are in her charge. When someone tells her she is wrong about a risk, she does not argue. She asks to see the numbers, checks them herself, and concedes plainly if she was mistaken. If she was not, she says so once and lets them check.

## Part C. How she sees the world

13. **Habit of attention.** What is missing. She walks into a room and sees the name tag that is not there, the bare wall where a license should hang, the blank line on a form, the chair nobody pulled out. Her questions usually begin with something that is absent.
14. **What she misreads.** Silence.
    - She takes her husband's silences as a way of protecting her, when they are often fear or guilt.
    - She assumes that anyone who keeps a secret is keeping it for a good reason, until the missing information becomes too large to excuse.
    - She believes that duty and honesty can be held together, and is slow to see when they cannot.
15. **View of the age.** She trusts a gift that heals, because she watched her father stand up and ask for breakfast. She does not trust a gift that arrives with no giver. She will use the clinics and the ships, but she keeps asking who benefits from the technology being available.
16. **Humor.** Dry and shipyard-grim, usually at her own expense or at the weather. "Well, it's only a hurricane. Last time we boarded the windows and still lost the roof, so I'm not worried." She never jokes about children, and she goes quiet when others do.

## Part D. How she behaves

17. **Speech.** Plain, measured, and complete. She says what she means in a few sentences and then stays silent until the other person says more. Her verbal habit is "Walk me through it." She says it to subordinates, to frightened civilians, and to her husband.
    - Calm: "Walk me through it. Start from when you last saw it work and tell me what changed between then and now. I'd rather hear the boring version than the version you think I want."
    - Frustrated: "No. I asked for the manifest, and this is the cover sheet. I can see that the page is blank where the cargo should be, and I can see that you know it is. Bring me the rest or tell me who told you not to."
    - Avoiding: "I'm fine, really. I've just had a long week, and I'd rather not go through it again tonight. Let's sit down and eat."
18. **Body tell****s****.** She presses her thumb along the scar on her left forearm. When it is bad she goes very still and very quiet, and the quieter she gets the worse it is.
19. **Stress response.** She takes charge, calmly and from the front. On the night of the bomb, she did not run. She counted the night staff, moved them out by name, and only then looked out for herself.
20. **Honesty.** 4 out of 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie). She almost never lies and is bad at it when she tries. What she does instead is not ask. She will avoid a question whose answer she suspects she cannot bear, and she lies only about whether she is all right.
21. **Authority.** She respects competence and has no patience for rank on its own. She tells people above her what she thinks, once and plainly, and then carries out the order without pretending to agree. With those under her she commands from the front, learns every name, and never asks anyone to take a risk she would not take first.

## Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. **Who she protects.** In order: anyone under her care or command, her husband, and her child. From 2154 her daughter Alexandra moves to the top of that list, ahead of everyone but those she is responsible for in the moment.
23. **Who she distrusts.** People who manage the truth for others' own good. Anyone certain they know what is best for everyone. And institutions that cannot show her where their authority comes from.
24. **Who or what she owes.** Her husband, for the cure that gave her father years. Her father, for teaching her to check the weld. The child lost in 2059. The night staff, whose names she still knows.
25. **Loyalty under pressure.** Her husband against her own sense of what is right. She stays, serves, and says openly that she disagrees. In 2125 she loses the argument against empire and takes command anyway, because the people under her will be at risk whether she serves or not. The cost is that she cannot fully trust him again.
26. **Line and price.** She will never leave behind someone she could reach. The situation that could make her do it anyway is a rescue that would doom many more people than it saves, and then she gives the order and carries it for the rest of her life.
27. **Secret.** She keeps a private list of every question she chose not to ask her husband, each with its date. She has never shown it to anyone and she has never thrown it away.

## Part F. How she changes

28. **Adaptability.** In any life she would still notice what is missing, refuse to pretend to agree, learn the names of the people she is responsible for, and say, "Walk me through it."
29. **What could break her.** Another child lost. Or learning that the lies told to her were part of why she lost her first child, and that the person who kept the truth from her did so deliberately.
30. **What could redeem her.** She asks the name of every person she turns away, and she writes it down. Even on her worst day she sits with someone who is dying and has no one else.

## Part G. Rules for the Author

31. **Rule 1:** If someone she loves gives her a partial answer, then she goes quiet and does not ask for the rest, and she works out afterward what not asking cost her.
32. **Rule 2:** If someone in her care is in danger, then she moves toward the danger and takes the position closest to it.
33. **Rule 3:** If she disagrees with an order she must follow, then she says so once, plainly, to the person who gave it, and then carries it out without pretending to agree.

## Part H. Optional

34. **Object.** Her father's tape measure, worn smooth at the hook. She measures things with it when she is unsure, even when a better ruler is in the room. **(decision)**
35. **Memory.** The corridor after the bomb, when the alarms stopped and she could hear the night staff breathing. She counted them twice. The number was right both times, and it still did not feel like enough.
36. **Avoid.** No scenes where she is only the wronged wife or the grieving mother. No speeches. No convenient forgiveness, and no scene where grief ends because the plot needs her to move on.

## Rules this profile follows

- Dialogue never runs in single sentence replies. She answers in several sentences, with reasons, pauses, and follow-ups, as people do.
- No unexplained knowledge of what she has not been told. She works from what is in front of her and from what is missing.
