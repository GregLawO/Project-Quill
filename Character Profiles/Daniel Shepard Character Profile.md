# Daniel "Danny" Shepard: Character Profile

Anything I invented to fill a gap is marked **(decision)** and is yours to overrule. This profile follows the 2 November 2026 rulings in `Revision Proposal - Elite Income and the Secret War.md`, which are not yet applied to the outline or the bible. Danny is Tom and Ruth's adopted son, Hannah's adoptive older brother, one of Vance's first recruits, and the man who asks Kenji to save Tom in 2053. He dies in the 2059 bombing. He is a supporting character and carries no POV.

## Part A. Who he is

1. **Name.** Daniel Shepard, born Daniel Iglesias. Tom and Hannah call him "Danny," and Ruth says "Daniel" when she is worried. He dislikes "Sarge," which the men who served under him in the Army still use. He calls Kenji "sir" and Vance "Gunny." **(decision)**
2. **Birth year.** 2022. He is thirty-one in 2053, thirty-seven when he dies in 2059, and nine years older than Hannah. **(decision)**
3. **Region of origin.** Galveston, in the same shipyard district as the Shepards. His father, Ray Iglesias, was a welder on Tom's crew and died when a gantry cable failed in 2029. His mother died the following year, and Tom and Ruth took him in at eight. **(decision)** The old street smelled of salt, diesel, and welding smoke, and everyone knew whose father could fix what.
4. **Languages.** Spanish first, from his parents, and English with a Gulf Coast accent that the Army sanded down. He switches to Spanish with frightened people and when he is tired. **(decision)**
5. **Trade.** Best: Army combat engineer (sapper). He reads how a structure carries weight, where it will fail, and how to take it down or hold it up. Second-best: rigger, which he learned beside Tom as a boy.
6. **Money and standing.** From 2052 he draws a network wage and spends almost none of it. He owns his father's chipping hammer and little else. He sends a share home through Ruth without saying where it comes from. **(decision)**
7. **Appearance.**
   - Broad shoulders and a plain, tired face that no longer matches his age after treatment, and an Army engineer's castle tattooed on the inside of his left wrist.
   - A leg that works perfectly, but he still lets the left foot trail on stairs, a habit from before.
   - He stands with his weight low, like a man expecting a load to shift.

## Part B. What drives him

8. **Small want.** To see his adoptive father walk out of the frosted doors, and then, each day, to have his post covered and the people near it safe.
9. **Large want.** To finish his seven-year term in autumn 2059 and stay anyway, by choice, and to be the brother Hannah thinks he is and not the man who left. He would not say either aloud. **(decision)**
10. **Fear.** Being absent when someone he is responsible for needs him. He will not leave a post uncovered unless someone in his family is in danger.
11. **Wound.** In 2047 a collapsed building crushed his legs and killed two men under his charge. He lived, was discharged, and left Galveston in 2048 without telling anyone why. The injury taught him that structures fail and that people stand under them. The leaving taught him that silence is easier than explaining. **(decision)**
12. **Pride.** Reading a structure: what is holding it up and what would bring it down. When someone says he is wrong about a load, he does not argue. He walks to it, puts his palm on it, and either says "Come look" or says "You're right" and fixes it.

## Part C. How he sees the world

13. **Habit of attention.** What is carrying the weight, and what would bring it down. He walks into a room and sees which post holds the ceiling, which truck is riding low on its springs, and which stranger is standing where a collapse would reach someone he cares about. This is a different habit from Vance, who reads ground for defense, and from Tom, who reads hands and tools.
14. **What he misreads.** His own value to his family.
    - He thinks he is welcome only when he is useful, so he stays away when he feels useless.
    - He assumes Hannah is better off not knowing where he works, and he underrates how much a silence from him costs her.
    - He reads fear in himself as weakness, and does not see that it is why people trust his judgment of a load.
15. **View of the age.** Grateful and uneasy. The machine gave him his legs back, and he knows what he owes. He also sees that the whole organization runs on people who cannot say no to it, and he is quietly glad that he chose to stay.
16. **Humor.** Dry, sapper-dry, aimed at himself or at a bad design. "If it holds, it's a design. If it doesn't, it's a lesson." He never jokes about the two men he lost.

## Part D. How he behaves

17. **Speech.** Even, low, and short. He explains a problem by pointing at it, and he does not fill silences. His verbal habit is "That's load-bearing," said about anything he thinks must not be disturbed.
    - Calm: "Walk it with me. I want to see what's holding up this roof before anybody puts a ladder on it. If something's wrong, it'll be at the joint, and I'd rather find it with my hand than with my head."
    - Frustrated: "No. That's load-bearing, and you've got a man standing under it. Take him out from there now. I'm not angry that you didn't see it, I'm angry that nobody asked."
    - Lying or avoiding: "It's a contractor job in Houston. Mostly gates and fences, the same as it was. Don't worry about me, Mom, just eat."
18. **Body tell.** He lays a flat palm against the nearest wall or post, as if testing it. When it is bad, he stays pressed against it and will not step away.
19. **Stress response.** He takes the heavy job and puts himself under the load. In 2047 he held a beam over two men for forty seconds so they could crawl out, and both died anyway. **(decision)**
20. **Honesty.** 3 out of 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie). He is a conditioned recruit, so he cannot tell his family what he does, and he turns that into small evasions. He never invents a story for Hannah that he cannot defend. He lies about where he is stationed and about whether he is all right.
21. **Authority.** He obeys Vance without argument and is polite and exact with Kenji. With younger guards he is patient and teaches them to read a floor before they stand on it.

## Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. **Who he protects.** In order: Tom and Ruth, Hannah, and the post he is assigned to. After 2056 Hannah moves to the top.
23. **Who he distrusts.** Inspectors and officials who sign off on a structure without touching it, and helpful strangers who turn up near Hannah.
24. **Who or what he owes.** Tom and Ruth, who took him in. Kenji, for his legs, which he repays with seven years. The two men he lost in 2047.
25. **Loyalty under pressure.** Kenji's order of silence against his family's need to know. He chooses the order, and it costs him Hannah's trust when she asks him straight out where he works and he gives her an answer that says nothing. **(decision)**
26. **Line and price.** He will never leave a post uncovered. The one thing that could make him break it is someone in his family in danger, and in 2059 it does.
27. **Secret.** Since 2056, on Kenji's off-the-books order, he has watched over Hannah from outside her security roster, in the car park and on the road, without telling her. He has also decided to stay after his term ends, and has told no one. **(decision)**

## Part F. How he changes

28. **Adaptability.** In any life he would still read what is holding a thing up, say "That's load-bearing," and put his hand on the wall first.
29. **What could break him.** Hannah or Tom hurt because of his silence, or because he was not there.
30. **What could redeem him.** He stays late to teach a new guard to read a floor, and he sends money home with a note that says only "for the roof."

## Part G. Rules for the Author

31. **Rule 1:** If something looks wrong with how a thing is holding weight, then he puts his hand on it and checks it before he says a word.
32. **Rule 2:** If someone in his family is in danger, then he leaves whatever he is guarding and goes toward them.
33. **Rule 3:** If someone asks him straight out something he cannot answer, then he gives a small true answer that says nothing and changes the subject to a task.

## Part H. Optional

34. **Object.** His father's chipping hammer, the head worn smooth, kept in his duty bag. **(decision)**
35. **Memory.** Standing under a gantry at eight years old while Tom showed him the cable and said, "We check it twice, so nobody has to ask why we didn't."
36. **Avoid.** No saintly martyr, and no scene where he exists only to be mourned. He appears alive in at least two scenes before 2059 so the death costs something. No speeches, and no scene where he is only comic relief. **(decision)**

## Rules this profile follows

- Dialogue never runs in single-sentence replies. He answers in several sentences, starting with the thing in front of him.
- He knows only what he has seen and been told. He is a conditioned recruit and cannot tell his family what he does.
- He carries no POV.
