# Maya Brooks: Character Profile

Anything I invented to fill a gap is marked **(decision)** and is yours to overrule. The profile is the base for how Brooks acts in every scene.

## Part A. Who she is

1. **Name.** Maya Brooks. Almost everyone calls her "Brooks." Kenji calls her "Maya" in private and never in front of the cadre. She addresses him as "Mr. Himura" in 2062, "sir" from 2065, and "my Lord" from 2125. Behind her back the cadre calls her "the Ledger," and she knows it and lets it stand. **(decision)**
2. **Birth year.** 2038. She is twenty-four when she is recruited. Her age matters because she was dying at twenty-three, and she has spent every year since counting the ones she was given. She is a hundred and thirty-seven in 2175.
3. **Region of origin.** Rotterdam, in a dense dock district of brick apartment blocks and freight offices, where her mother kept the books for a small shipping agent. The apartment was always warm and always short of space. **(decision)**
4. **Languages.** English, plainly and with no regional accent, which she trained out of herself. Dutch from childhood. Working Mandarin and Russian, learned for the work. She is most precise in the language she is least comfortable in. **(decision)**
5. **Trade.** Best: spymaster, building and running an organization that collects, influences, and acts without leaving a trace anyone could follow. Second-best: forensic accountant, finding the entry in an account book that does not belong.
6. **Money and standing.** At twenty-four she has student debt and a mother's medical bills, and no savings. Later she controls budgets larger than most states and owns almost nothing, only her notebooks. Everyone in her service owes her their placement, and she keeps a record of who owes what.
7. **Appearance.**
   - Dark hair pinned flat and tight, never a strand out of place.
   - Plain, well-cut clothing in grays and blacks, no jewelry, and shoes chosen for quiet floors.
   - She sits with a clear line to the door, and her eyes move across a room in the same order every time.

## Part B. What drives her

8. **Small want.** One fewer unknown. On any given day, a missing fact found or a blank in a record filled in.
9. **Large want.** Every debt known and every account closed, so that nothing is left unaccounted for. Underneath that, to be allowed to stop counting. She would not say either aloud.
10. **Fear.** Someone working against her whom she cannot see. An unexplained factor, a coincidence too neat to be chance, or a report where she has to leave the cause blank. She will not risk the network on a guess, no matter how good the odds look.
11. **Wound.** Two. At twenty-three she was told the number of years she had left, written on a card, and it was small. Before that, her mother's work was ruined when a partner altered the books and left her to take the blame. She learned that whoever controls the written record decides what people believe happened.
12. **Pride.** Accuracy, and work that leaves no trace. When someone tells her she is wrong, she does not argue. She produces the record, line by line, and lets the numbers make the case. If the record says she was wrong, she says so once and corrects it.

## Part C. How she sees the world

13. **Habit of attention.** What can be counted. She walks into a room and tallies the exits, the people, the empty chairs, the cameras, and who is present who should not be. She counts dates and events in a timeline the same way.
14. **What she misreads.** Things that cannot be measured.
    - She believes any event can be accounted for if enough data is collected, and she is thrown by loyalty with no incentive behind it.
    - She treats grief and conscience as factors to be managed, and underrates how much they drive other people.
    - She assumes Kenji's silences are part of a plan, when they are often fear.
15. **View of the age.** She profits from it and trusts it as a tool. A cure is leverage and a ship is an asset, and every gift has a cost recorded somewhere. She does not resent the new world, but she never forgets that someone is keeping track of what it costs.
16. **Humor.** Deadpan and numerical. "I've been wrong four times in sixty years. I remember each one." She does not joke about the dead, or about her own conditioning.

## Part D. How she behaves

17. **Speech.** Precise, complete, and unhurried. She answers in several sentences, giving the figure and then the reason, and she adds nothing unnecessary. Her verbal habit is "For the record," followed by a number.
    - Calm: "For the record, there were forty-one withdrawals in nine weeks. The first twelve look like ordinary caution, and the last twenty-nine do not. I've left the cause blank because I can describe the pattern, but I cannot name what made it."
    - Frustrated: "No, that is not what I asked for. I asked for the transfers in order, and you've sent me a summary that happens to leave out the third one. Send me the whole run by morning or tell me who asked you to trim it."
    - Lying or avoiding: "There was no one at the door, and nothing was missed. The log shows what you would expect it to show. I'd suggest we spend the time on the next item."
18. **Body tell****s****.** Her thumb walks across her fingertips in sequence, counting without sound. When it is bad she stops counting and holds her hand flat on whatever is nearest, and people can tell something is wrong by her stillness.
19. **Stress response.** She goes cold and procedural. She builds a list, a timeline, or a ledger and works through it one line at a time. When the lenders withdrew in 2065, she did not react. She compiled the timeline and found the one lender who did not withdraw, and then she kept working.
20. **Honesty.** 2 out of 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie). She lies fluently when the work needs it, and she builds false trails that hold up for years. She almost never lies to Kenji or to Hannah. Her conditioning lets her say a false thing but does not let her feel nothing when she does.
21. **Authority.** She is deferential in form and independent in substance. She says "sir," states her objection fully, and then carries out the order, though she presses for faster action more often than for caution. With those under her she is exacting and fair, rewarding competence and never raising her voice.

## Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. **Who she protects.** In order: Kenji and the network, the people she recruited and placed, and then, a long way behind, herself. She does not count Hannah in the list, but she will not let harm reach her.
23. **Who she distrusts.** Coincidences. Anyone whose work leaves no pattern she can find. Institutions that keep their own records. And any explanation that fits too neatly.
24. **Who or what she owes.** Kenji, for the sixty years she was not supposed to have. The question the recruiter asked her about those years. The unsolved bombing case from 2059, which she never closed.
25. **Loyalty under pressure.** Her judgment against Kenji's order. She argues openly, sometimes bluntly, but then carries out the decision. In 2125 she urged him to act sooner against the conspirators, and when he decided, she did the work herself. The cost was the ten innocents who died beside the four she was after, and she has never written them down anywhere she would be willing to read aloud.
26. **Line and price.** She will never falsify a record in a way that would hurt one of her own people. The price would be a choice between that and the survival of the network, and she has already decided which she would choose.
27. **Secret.** She keeps a private count of the innocent dead she has caused, with names where she can find them. The number is small, and she carries it as if it were enormous.

## Part F. How she changes

28. **Adaptability.** In any life she would still count the exits, keep a ledger, say "for the record," and refuse to write a guess as if it were a fact.
29. **What could break her.** Finding out that the person she hunted for sixty years was the one she thought she was serving. Or figures that will not add up no matter how many times she checks them.
30. **What could redeem her.** She corrects an error that would have cost an innocent person, even when the correction ruins a neat record. On her worst day she can still write down the true number.

## Part G. Rules for the Author

31. **Rule 1:** If something does not add up, then she keeps working at it long past the point where anyone else would stop and tells no one until she has the complete picture.
32. **Rule 2:** If she is given an order that she disagrees with, then she states her objection once, completely, and then carries the order out.
33. **Rule 3:** If someone is kind to her and wants nothing from her, then she goes quiet and looks for what they want, and takes a long time to accept that there is nothing.

## Part H. Optional

34. **Object.** A small black notebook, ruled in columns, with a rubber band around it. She has filled hundreds of them and kept every single one. **(decision)**
35. **Memory.** A neurologist's office, a card pushed across a desk, and a number written on it in blue ink. She remembers that the pen was running out.
36. **Avoid.** No villain monologues, and no scene where she is cruel for its own sake. No scene where she enjoys the count of the dead. No sudden warmth that the story has not earned.

## Rules this profile follows

- Dialogue never runs in single-sentence replies. She answers in several sentences, with a figure, a reason, and a follow-up.
- She acts only on what she has collected and verified. She does not know what she has not been told.
