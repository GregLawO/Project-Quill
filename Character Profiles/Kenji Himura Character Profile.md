# Kenji Himura: Character Profile

Anything I invented to fill a gap is marked **(decision)** and is yours to overrule. This profile includes private facts (the transmigration, the Reapers from 2065) that no other character knows.

## Part A. Who he is

1. **Name.** Kenji Himura. Hannah, the Shepards, and the found family call him "Kenji." Tom adds "son" in private. Brooks says "Mr. Himura" in 2062, "sir" from 2065, and "my Lord" from 2125. He has no nickname. The one name he dislikes is "my Lord," which he accepts because the empire needs one person at the top to carry the title. **(decision)**
2. **Birth year.** 2020 (the body), so he is thirty when he wakes in 2050. His age matters because his body is thirty but the mind inside it is older. How old that mind is has not been decided.
3. **Region of origin.** **(decision)** He wakes in a small flat in the Houston–Galveston shipyard belt, in a body that is not his own. The district is working-class, with cranes, tankers, and cheap rent, and it puts him a short walk from Tom Shepard's yard. The past life he remembers is a crowded, poor district with a quiet apartment, where someone else's footsteps always passed his door.
4. **Languages.** English, plainly spoken and without accent. A few Japanese words come out when he is exhausted, mostly numbers and "ah." **(decision)** He talks the way a patient, practical man does: a few sentences at a time, with a reason and often a follow-up question, never a bare one-line answer. He rarely uses comparisons, and the ones he uses come from workshops and tools.
5. **Trade.** Best: builder of production systems (factories, the tools that build the tools, and the supply lines behind them). Second-best: clinical technician, since the Med-Pod knowledge is full expertise.
6. **Money and standing.** In 2050 he has nothing but his hands. From 2053 the clinics pay, and by 2065 he controls an empire through layered holdings. He owns almost nothing in his own name, only his tools. Almost everyone in his network owes him, but he feels that he owes them, not the other way round.
7. **Appearance.**
   - His hands are scrubbed pink, with short nails and chapped knuckles.
   - He wears plain dark workshop clothes with no logos, and a good jacket only when forced.
   - He stands at the edge of rooms near a door, and he sits with his back to the wall, a habit Vance taught him. He looks at what is in your hands before he looks at your face.

## Part B. What drives him

8. **Small want.** To finish the task in front of him before the next one lands on his desk. In 2065 it is one signed commitment before dawn.
9. **Large want.** A quiet dinner with Hannah where nobody needs anything from him. Underneath that, to have someone tell him he can stop working. He would not say either aloud.
10. **Fear.** Losing the people he has. Before 2065 it is a cautious man's unease about the unknown. From 2065 it is the Reapers, and he never talks about it and only makes long task lists. He will not risk the found family or Hannah, no matter how good the odds look.
11. **Wound.** A poor, lonely childhood in the past life, which is why being wanted makes him flinch and he finishes every meal. The second wound is the 2059 bomb that killed the child.
12. **Pride.** Making things that work. When told he is wrong, he does not argue. He asks "What's it for?" and tests it. If he was wrong, he says so in one sentence and fixes it.

## Part C. How he sees the world

13. **Habit of attention.** What a thing is for, and what it will be used for next. He walks into a room and sees the chair as a lever, the lamp cord as eleven feet of rope, and a stranger's briefcase as something the stranger has already decided to use.
14. **What he misreads.** Being cared for, and bad luck.
    - He assumes care must be paid for, and that people serve from gratitude or conditioning, not choice.
    - He reads coincidence as hostility. He feels the universe working against him, though chance or human causes could explain what happens.
    - He underrates how much of Hannah's dissent is love.
15. **View of the age.** He trusts the technology completely and the people who will use it very little. He built the technology and knows exactly what it is for. He admires the God-Emperor, a ruler from a story he once read, for saving mankind, and criticizes him for failing it. In 2051 he decides that only a weaker, public version of any technology may leave his workshop, so that no one can trace it back to him.
16. **Humor.** Rare, dry, and understated, usually aimed at himself or at a tool. "Tools do what you point them at. That's the whole problem with them." He never jokes about the dead.

## Part D. How he behaves

17. **Speech.** He asks "What do you need?" more often than he gives orders. He never says "it can't get worse." He notices when things happen earlier or later than expected, but he never calls it luck, good or bad.
    - Calm: "What do you need? Tell me what's missing and when you need it by, and I'll work backward from there. If it's more than one thing, give me the one that's holding up the rest."
    - Frustrated: "Then do it again, and slowly this time. I'm not angry that it failed. I'm angry that nobody checked the seal before we pressurized, and now we've lost a day we didn't have."
    - Avoiding: "It's friends' money, Hannah. People who owed me favors, mostly, and who'd rather not be named. I'd tell you more if there were more worth telling."
18. **Body tell****s****.** He squares objects on a desk (the pen, his shoes on the mat) and washes his hands, twice when it is bad. At the worst moments his hands stay flat on the desk and he cannot lift them.
19. **Stress response.** He takes charge, quietly. He finishes the task in front of him before letting himself think about what has happened. In 2059, the night of the bomb, he hears the oath in the corridor and opens a blank file and writes the date.
20. **Honesty.** 4 out of 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie). He almost never says a false thing, but he withholds constantly. He hides how he knows what he knows, where the money comes from, and later the ten innocents of 2125. He never tells Hannah an outright falsehood, only true sentences that answer nothing.
21. **Authority.** Nobody is above him. With the Shepards he is almost deferential, and being called "son" by Tom unsettles him completely. With those under him he asks what they need and delegates. He is uneasy that asking "what is it for" can turn people into tools in his mind, and he catches himself doing it.

## Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. **Who he protects.** In order: Hannah, the found family (the first dozen servants and the Shepards), then humanity's survival. From 2154 Alexandra sits beside Hannah at the top.
23. **Who he distrusts.** Anyone who hopes a single hero will save everyone, because he knows how that usually ends. Whoever made the lenders withdraw in 2065, who he suspects is working against him. Institutions. And his own knowledge, since he never learns where it came from.
24. **Who or what he owes.** Tom, who calls him "son." Hannah, who stayed. Vance, who traded his memories for treatment and became the first member of his network. The child lost in 2059. From 2125, the ten innocents.
25. **Loyalty under pressure.** His secret plan against Hannah's wish for openness. In 2065 he asks her to move work farther from Sol and says "farther" twice. She does not ask why, and from then on she trusts him a little less. In 2125 he carries out the operation despite her arguments against empire, and ten innocent people die with it.
26. **Line and price.** He will never put a directive in Hannah or the found family, and Hannah and Alexandra receive the full Primaris enhancement without the loyalty conditioning. He would only break this if he had proof that conditioning was the only way to stop the Reapers from taking control of them. **(decision)**
27. **Secret.** He is a transmigrator who read Mass Effect fan novels, and he knows the Reapers are coming. No character ever learns the first, and only the inner circle learns the second, much later.

## Part F. How he changes

28. **Adaptability.** In any life he would still choose patience over ambition, finish every meal, ask "What do you need?", and want a quiet dinner with one person.
29. **What could break him.** Losing Alexandra as he lost the first child, or Hannah leaving once she knows what he did in 2125.
30. **What could redeem him.** Once, he folds a child's letter into his pocket while answering no one. Even on his worst day he asks someone what they need and means it.

## Part G. Rules for the Author

31. **Rule 1:** If someone near him is in trouble, then he asks what they need before he asks what happened.
32. **Rule 2:** If he is afraid, then he makes a list and starts the first item, and the fear shows only in how fast he works.
33. **Rule 3:** If someone thanks him, wants something from him, or tells him he is loved, then he changes the subject to a task and starts straightening something on his desk.

## Part H. Optional

34. **Object.** A child's letter, folded small in his pocket. **(decision)**
35. **Memory.** In the past life, eating dinner for one and finishing every bite because leaving food was a luxury, while a pair of footsteps on the stairs never stopped at his door. The later memory is the corridor in 2059.
36. **Avoid.** No smug-genius scenes, and no scene where he enjoys being thanked. No character is ever told where his knowledge came from. Past-life references stay under three sentences per chapter and come only through small habits, like finishing every meal. He may name his dread once per chapter at most.

## Rules this profile follows

- No Reaper thoughts and no past-fiction thoughts before 2065.

* Dialogue never runs in single-sentence replies. Characters answer in several sentences, with reasons, hesitations, and follow-ups, as people do.
