# Dominic Vance: Character Profile

Anything I invented to fill a gap is marked **(decision)** and is yours to overrule. The name and rank are placeholders. The profile is the base for how Vance acts in every scene.

## Part A. Who he is

1. **Name.** Dominic Vance, Gunnery Sergeant, United States Marine Corps, retired. **(decision)** The network's operatives call him "Gunny." Kenji calls him "Gunny" too. Brooks calls him "Sergeant" at first and "Vance" later. He dislikes "sir" and says so: "Don't call me sir. I work for a living."
2. **Birth year.** 2002. **(decision)** He is forty-nine when he is dying in a hospice in 2051, and he is in his late fifties when he recruits Brooks. His age matters because he spent his best years on a job that left him with ruined lungs and a small pension, and the cure gives him years he had written off.
3. **Region of origin.** Jacksonville, North Carolina, a base town next to a Marine camp, where half the street served and the other half sold things to those who did. **(decision)**
4. **Languages.** English with an eastern Carolina accent that flattens when he is working. Some Spanish and a few phrases in other languages from his deployments. He talks in plain, short military terms, and he explains them if the listener is a civilian.
5. **Trade.** Best: small-unit trainer and site defender, someone who teaches people how to hold ground and then holds it with them. Second-best: range safety officer. **(decision)**
6. **Money and standing.** At the start he has a small pension and a large medical debt, and nothing to pay with but what is in his head. Later the network pays him well and he spends almost none of it. People he trained owe him their lives, and he never mentions it.
7. **Appearance.**
   - A broad, heavy frame, gray buzz-cut hair, and a scar across the knuckles of his left hand.
   - A gray coat he wears even in July, because it hides the sidearm he will not go into a room without.
   - He breathes deeply and slowly, and he notices every breath, because he once could not.

## Part B. What drives him

8. **Small want.** The next site secured before the rollout date.
9. **Large want.** A unit again: people to look after and train. Underneath that, to die on his feet and not in a bed. He would not say either aloud.
10. **Fear.** A threat he cannot see. He can hold any ground he can walk, but he cannot defend people against an attack that comes through paper, money, or a lawyer's office. He will not leave a place he has promised to hold.
11. **Wound.** His squad walked into an ambush on bad intelligence, and he came home with the survivors. He was later dying in a bed with no one to pay for his care. **(decision)** The first taught him that the people on the ground pay for other people's mistakes. The second taught him that being useful is the only thing that gets you treated.
12. **Pride.** Training. His people do not die. When someone tells him he is wrong about a position, he does not argue. He asks to walk the ground, looks at it himself, and says plainly whether the other person was right.

## Part C. How he sees the world

13. **Habit of attention.** What can be defended and what cannot. He walks into a room and reads the ground: where the cover is, where the sight lines run, where he would stand to hold the room, and which side the door opens. He reads it with his body and counts nothing.
14. **What he misreads.** Threats that are not physical.
    - He expects an enemy to arrive with weapons, and he is slow to see an attack made of contracts and money.
    - He assumes that people who stay do so out of loyalty to the unit, when many stay out of gratitude or fear.
    - He underrates how much of Kenji's caution is fear, and sees it as discipline.
15. **View of the age.** He is grateful and wary. The treatment gave him his lungs back, so he considers himself in debt, but he sees every gift as a weapon in someone else's hands. He is both proud and uneasy that his own way of moving now lives in the machines, and he sometimes watches the sentries train at night.
16. **Humor.** Dry and gallows-style, usually at his own expense. "I've been shot at on four continents, and this is the first time I've been told to smile for a photograph." He does not joke about the dead.

## Part D. How he behaves

17. **Speech.** Plain and economical, but complete. He speaks in a few sentences, gives the position first and the reason second, and does not decorate. His verbal habit is "Walk the ground," which means go and look at it yourself before you decide.
    - Calm: "Walk the ground with me before we talk about the fence. I want to see where a man would stand to watch the door, and where I'd stand to stop him. Then we'll know what we need."
    - Frustrated: "No. You put the only entrance behind a blind corner, and now I have to guard it with two people instead of one. Move the desk, move the camera, and do it before dark, or else I'll be putting a man there who'll have no place to see from. Which will not be happening on my watch."
    - Lying or avoiding: "It's nothing. Just an old habit. I'm fine, and you can stop looking at me like that."
18. **Body tell****s****.** He takes a slow breath through his nose and his shoulders drop. When it is bad his right hand rests on the grip of the sidearm at his hip, without drawing it, and he does not take the hand away until the room is clear.
19. **Stress response.** He takes a position. He goes quiet, puts himself between the danger and the people he is responsible for, and moves everyone else to better ground. When a crew broke into the first warehouse, he was not there, and he has never forgotten it.
20. **Honesty.** 4 out of 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie). He almost never lies, and he is bad at it. He keeps secrets the way a soldier does: he says nothing and does not volunteer. He kept the machine's secret for months, even from the people who treated him.
21. **Authority.** He respects competence and has no patience for rank without it. He gives clear, short orders and never asks anyone to do what he would not do. He works for Kenji because Kenji pays and listens, not because Kenji outranks him. He is the one who taught Kenji where to sit in a room.

## Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. **Who he protects.** In order: the people he trained and sent out, Kenji, and Brooks, whom he recruits and then watches over. Later his job narrows to guarding one man, the man Brooks serves, and he does not mind.
23. **Who he distrusts.** People who walk into a room without looking at the exits. People who give orders from far away. And anyone who claims a threat is "only paperwork."
24. **Who or what he owes.** Kenji, for his lungs and his years. The men of his old squad. The person who recruited him into the dying, and who he never learned to thank.
25. **Loyalty under pressure.** His duty to the people on the ground against the secrecy he agreed to. He chose the secrecy, and he kept it even when it meant not telling the hospice staff that the cure worked. The cost was that people he could have helped died while he said nothing. **(decision)**
26. **Line and price.** He will never abandon a position he promised to hold while people are behind him. The situation that could make him break it is an order that would save many more people by giving the ground up, and then he gives the order himself and carries it.
27. **Secret.** He goes to watch the sentries train at night. Their movements are his own, copied from his memories, and those memories include how his dead squadmates moved, so watching the sentries is the only way he still sees those men in motion.

## Part F. How he changes

28. **Adaptability.** In any life he would still read the exits first, train whoever is willing, take the last watch, and say "Walk the ground."
29. **What could break him.** Losing the people he trained to a threat he never saw coming. Or learning that the secrecy he protected was what killed them.
30. **What could redeem him.** He teaches anyone who asks, including frightened clerks, how to stand in a doorway and where to go if shots start getting fired. He stays until the last person is out.

## Part G. Rules for the Author

31. **Rule 1:** If he enters a room, then he finds the exits and the best position before he sits down.
32. **Rule 2:** If someone in his care is in danger, then he puts himself between them and the danger.
33. **Rule 3:** If he is told to guard something without being told what the threat is, then he asks to walk the ground first and says what he would need.

## Part H. Optional

34. **Object.** A set of dog tags from his old squad, kept in the pocket of the gray coat. **(decision)**
35. **Memory.** The gully before the ambush, and where each man was standing. He does not count them. He remembers the places.
36. **Avoid.** No scene where he is only muscle or a tough-guy cliché. No scene where he explains the setting. No scene where he is smarter than everyone about money or law.

## Rules this profile follows

- Dialogue never runs in single-sentence replies. He answers in several sentences, position first and reason second.
- He is not conditioned in his early years with Kenji, and he stays because he chooses to. Only later, when he joins Kenji's personal guard, does he receive the conditioning that makes betrayal unthinkable.
- He does not count anything. His attention is ground, cover, and sight lines.
