# Ruth Shepard: Character Profile

Anything I invented to fill a gap is marked **(decision)** and is yours to overrule. The profile is the base for how Ruth acts in every scene.

## Part A. Who she is

1. **Name.** Ruth Shepard. Tom calls her "Ruth," and "Ruthie" when he is trying to get out of an argument. Hannah calls her "Mom." Kenji calls her "Ma'am" before the wedding and "Mom" after it. She dislikes "Mama Shepard," which some of the younger teachers call her behind her back. **(decision)**
2. **Birth year.** 2003. She is forty-seven in 2050 and fifty when her husband is cured. She lives to 116 on the clinics' public treatment. Her age matters because she has been a school administrator long enough to have taught the children of her former students.
3. **Region of origin.** Galveston, Texas, in a school district she has never left. She grew up in a house where her mother looked after everyone else, yet who didn't even look after her mother in return. **(decision)**
4. **Languages.** English with a soft Gulf Coast accent. Working Spanish, learned from the parents of her students. She uses a gentler tone for a crying child or a frightened parent than for a board meeting.
5. **Trade.** Best: school administrator, someone who runs a school and knows every child's name and what is going on at home. Second-best: hospital patient advocate, the kind of job where a calm person sits with frightened families and finds out what they need. **(decision)**
6. **Money and standing.** At forty-seven she has a steady salary and a husband's medical debt. Later the debt is gone and she spends little. She owns her house and her good shoes. Parents and former students owe her small favors, and she never mentions them.
7. **Appearance.**
   - Small and straight-backed, with short gray hair and reading glasses pushed up on her head.
   - A cardigan in every season, and a ring of school keys on her belt, each labelled in her handwriting.
   - She sits where she can see the door and the faces at once, and she looks at people's hands as they speak.

## Part B. What drives her

8. **Small want.** Every child in the building looked after before the day ends.
9. **Large want.** To see her daughter happy and safe, with someone beside her who stays. Underneath that, to be told what is really going on in her family. She would not say either aloud.
10. **Fear.** Being helpless while someone she loves is in trouble. She will not leave a child in her building unwatched, whatever the rules say.
11. **Wound.** Two. She watched her mother wear herself out looking after everyone and die tired, with no one looking after her. **(decision)** Then she watched her husband nearly die and could do nothing but make tea. The first taught her that care has to run in both directions. The second taught her that she hates standing by.
12. **Pride.** Reading people. She can tell within a day who is being looked after and who is not. When someone tells her she is wrong about a child or a family, she does not argue. She asks "Tell me what you saw," listens fully, and says plainly if she was mistaken.

## Part C. How she sees the world

13. **Habit of attention.** Who looks after whom. She walks into a room and sees who carries whose bag, who steps aside for whom, who eats last, who cuts a child's food, and who watches the door on someone else's behalf. She notices who is not being looked after.
14. **What she misreads.** Care that is used as a tool.
    - She assumes people who look after others are good people, and is slow to see that care can be a way of controlling someone.
    - She reads a man's devotion as proof of his honesty.
    - She sees that Kenji is paying a price, but she assumes it is private grief and not a plan.
15. **View of the age.** She is grateful and watchful. The clinics gave her husband his life back, so she uses them, but she refuses the knowledge upload for herself and keeps wondering who is paying for all of it. She does not distrust the new world, but she keeps asking who looks after the people who built it.
16. **Humor.** Warm and teasing, usually about children and schools. "Children are the only honest people in this building, and they lie constantly." She never jokes about a child in trouble.

## Part D. How she behaves

17. **Speech.** Warm, patient, and complete. She talks in full sentences, asks gentle questions, and waits for the answer. Her verbal habit is "And who is looking after you?" She asks it of students, parents, her husband, and her daughter.
    - Calm: "Sit down, and tell me what happened from the beginning. You don't have to make it sound better than it was. When you're done, I'll tell you what I think, and then we'll decide what to do about it."
    - Frustrated: "No. I have listened to the board for an hour, and I have not heard one person say how many children will be in that room. Until somebody can tell me, I'm not signing anything, and I'd like that written in the minutes."
    - Lying or avoiding: "Oh, I'm perfectly fine. I've just been on my feet all day, and it's catching up with me. Let's not talk about it. Have you eaten?"
18. **Body tell.** She straightens a napkin or a collar that does not need straightening. When it is bad she folds her hands in her lap and holds one wrist with the other hand, and she does not move.
19. **Stress response.** She takes care of the people around her. She sits them down, puts something to drink in their hands, and asks quiet questions. When Tom was diagnosed she made tea for everyone in the room and organized the paperwork. She did not cry until she was alone in the car.
20. **Honesty.** 4 out of 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie). She almost never lies. She says kind things that are also true, and she chooses carefully which true thing to say. She lies only to comfort a frightened child, and she knows she is doing it. She asked Kenji "who are you?" once, got an answer that was true and told her nothing, and chose not to ask again.
21. **Authority.** She is polite and unmovable with people above her. She disagrees in plain words and does not raise her voice. With staff she is warm, fair, and hard to fool. She respects anyone who treats children well and has no time for anyone who does not.

## Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. **Who she protects.** In order: Hannah, Tom, and the children in her school. Kenji is family, and she protects him quietly, though she would not put him ahead of the others.
23. **Who she distrusts.** People who are kind because it is useful. People who are very polite at the door of a school. And officials who arrive with a good lawyer and no reason.
24. **Who or what she owes.** Kenji, for Tom's life. Her mother, who looked after everyone. The grandchild lost in 2059.
25. **Loyalty under pressure.** Her daughter's marriage against her own question. She asked Kenji who he is, and then she kept the answer to herself, because she did not want to put her own doubt between Hannah and her husband. The cost is that she carries it alone, and her doubt has not gone away.
26. **Line and price.** She will never leave a child in her care without someone looking after them. What could make her break it is a rule that would close the whole school, and then she breaks the smaller rule and takes the blame.
27. **Secret.** She has noticed that her family is being watched over by strangers: a man by the back door, a machine in the car park, a stranger who is always nearby. She has told no one, not even Tom.

## Part F. How she changes

28. **Adaptability.** In any life she would still notice who looks after whom, ask "And who is looking after you?", and refuse to leave a child unwatched.
29. **What could break her.** A child in her care being hurt because she did not ask the question in time. Or learning that her daughter was hurt because she kept her doubt to herself.
30. **What could redeem her.** She brings food to the person nobody sent for. She remembers the name of every student and every parent, and she uses them.

## Part G. Rules for the Author

31. **Rule 1:** If someone is with a child or a vulnerable person, then she watches how they treat them and judges them by it.
32. **Rule 2:** If someone is hurting, then she sits them down and gives them something to eat or drink before she asks a single question.
33. **Rule 3:** If a decision would harm a child in her care, then she says no, politely and firmly, in front of the people who want a yes, and does not give way.

## Part H. Optional

34. **Object.** The ring of school keys, each labelled in her own handwriting, and the one at the end labelled "spare, ask me." **(decision)**
35. **Memory.** Her mother washing dishes late at night, after everyone had eaten and gone to bed, and no one noticing.
36. **Avoid.** No scene where she is only a worried mother or only a comfort to others. No scene where she is a victim. No scene where she works out the secrets by herself, and no long speeches.

## Rules this profile follows

- Dialogue never runs in single-sentence replies. She answers in several sentences, warmly and completely.
- She knows only what she has seen and been told. She took the cure but refused the knowledge upload, so she has no extra knowledge and no conditioning.
- She does not know who is behind the clinics, or why.
