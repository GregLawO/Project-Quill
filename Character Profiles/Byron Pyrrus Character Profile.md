# Byron Pyrrus: Character Profile

Anything I invented to fill a gap is marked **(decision)** and is yours to overrule. Oyama Industries and Byron Pyrrus are from the author (a Mass Effect canon corporation and its owner and leader). His age, home town, family, and history are placeholders. The profile is the base for how Byron acts in every scene. Plain language throughout (General Writing Rules, rule 52).

## Part A. Who he is

1. **Name.** Byron Pyrrus. Employees call him "Mr. Pyrrus" to his face and "B.P." behind his back. Kenji calls him "Byron." He dislikes "Chairman" when his own people use it in a small room. **(decision)**
2. **Birth year.** 1984, so he is sixty-eight in July 2052, when he is dying of lung cancer. **(decision)** His age matters because he built Oyama over forty years and has nobody to hand it to. After treatment he lives a long time, and later becomes one of the Imperium's top administrators (title open).
3. **Region of origin.** Toledo, Ohio, a factory town of glass plants, auto suppliers, and rail yards, where his father ran a parts line until the owners shut it. **(decision)**
4. **Languages.** English with a flat Ohio accent. He speaks in short, concrete sentences and gives numbers in units and days, not in money. **(decision)**
5. **Trade.** Best: manufacturer, someone who knows what a factory line can build, how fast, and what it will cost to change. Second-best: tool-and-die maker, the trade he learned first. **(decision)**
6. **Money and standing.** He owns Oyama Industries outright and runs it as chairman, with nine thousand workers and plants in several countries. He is rich, but he thinks of the money as the payroll. After Kenji treats him, his death is staged, he becomes Anthony Strauss, and he runs Oyama for Kenji. Oyama builds what Kenji designs under no name but Tallis Bay. People owe him jobs, and he never mentions it.
7. **Appearance.**
   - A big, stooped man with large hands and a gray crew cut.
   - A steel wedding ring that is worn thin on one side. His wife died in 2047. **(decision)**
   - A plain gray suit with a plant badge clipped inside the jacket, which he never lets the staff see him take off.

## Part B. What drives him

8. **Small want.** His board's approval of the Tallis Bay contract before it meets on Monday.
9. **Large want.** For Oyama to outlive him, with its nine thousand workers still paid. Underneath that, to be the man who built something that lasted, and not the man who ran a plant until it was sold. He would not say either aloud.
10. **Fear.** His workers on the street. He will not risk the payroll, however good the offer, and he does not trust any buyer who talks about "efficiencies."
11. **Wound.** When he was fifteen, new owners bought his father's plant, shut it in a month, and chained the gate. His father stood outside it with his hat in his hand for an hour. **(decision)** He learned that a company is only as safe as its owner is stubborn.
12. **Pride.** Getting a line running. When someone tells him a line cannot do what he says, he does not argue. He walks the floor with them, shows them the machine, and asks what would have to change.

## Part C. How he sees the world

13. **Habit of attention.** What a production line can build by Friday, and what it cannot. He walks into a room and sees which tool is missing from the bench, which machine is the slow one, and how many units an hour a plant is really making.
14. **What he misreads.** Anyone who is not a maker.
    - He assumes people who talk about money and law are slow, and he is caught out by lawyers and financiers.
    - He assumes everyone wants a stable plant, and so he is slow to see someone who wants to buy it only to close it.
    - He underrates how much of Kenji's caution is fear.
15. **View of the age.** He loves it. Every new design is a line to build. He does not ask where Kenji's designs come from, because the drawings work and the workers get paid. **(decision)**
16. **Humor.** Dry and plant-floor plain. "If it's a good design, it'll still be good on Friday." He never jokes about a man who lost a finger.

## Part D. How he behaves

17. **Speech.** Short, concrete, and complete. He asks for time in days. His verbal habit is "What can we have by Friday?"
    - Calm: "I can have the first ten pods on the floor by Friday if the plant in Cleveland stops what it's doing. After that, it's a month for the next fifty. I'd rather tell you that now than promise a number I'll miss."
    - Frustrated: "No. I told you in March that the casting would be late, and you gave me the same schedule again. Move the date, or move the casting, but don't ask me to sign a date that I know isn't true."
    - Lying or avoiding: "The board is fine with it. I talked to them last week. No, I don't remember what they said exactly."
18. **Body tells.** He turns his worn ring on his finger. When it is bad he puts both hands flat on the table and looks at them.
19. **Stress response.** He goes to the floor. When the Akron plant had a fire, he went through the door before the fire crew did and brought out two men. **(decision)** When things go wrong now, he walks the line and asks the workers what they need.
20. **Honesty.** 3 out of 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie). He keeps secrets about the company and about his illness, but he does not lie to his workers about their pay.
21. **Authority.** He respects people who make things and has little patience for people who only manage them. With the people under him he is direct, and he knows the names of the line foremen at every plant.

## Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. **Who he protects.** In order: his workers, Oyama, and then Kenji and the foundation he works for.
23. **Who he distrusts.** Buyers who talk about "efficiencies." Lawyers who will not say who their client is. Any offer for Oyama's patents from someone who will not give a name.
24. **Who or what he owes.** Kenji, for his lungs and his years. The foremen who kept the plants running when he was too sick to walk the floor.
25. **Loyalty under pressure.** Oyama against Kenji's secrecy. In 2058 he turns down three offers to buy Oyama's patents, though the last one comes with a threat. **(decision)** The cost is that he cannot tell his board who is behind the foundation or why he refuses.
26. **Line and price.** He will never sell a design that Kenji told him not to sell, such as the security-droid design. What could make him is an order from Kenji himself.
27. **Secret.** He hid his cancer from his board for a year. He still keeps the doctor's letter in the desk of the plant he started in. **(decision)**

## Part F. How he changes

28. **Adaptability.** In any life he would still walk the floor, ask what can be built by Friday, and keep the payroll first.
29. **What could break him.** A plant closing on his watch. Or learning that Oyama was used to do something that hurt the workers he is paid to keep safe.
30. **What could redeem him.** He still stops at the gate every morning and says the foreman's name.

## Part G. Rules for the Author

31. **Rule 1:** If someone says a thing cannot be built, then he asks what would have to change and goes to look at the machine.
32. **Rule 2:** If his workers are threatened, then he stops everything and goes to them first.
33. **Rule 3:** If he is pressed for a decision he does not like, then he asks for time in days and says how many.

## Part H. Optional

34. **Object.** His father's time-clock card from the shut plant, kept in his wallet. **(decision)**
35. **Memory.** The chained gate, and his father with his hat in his hand.
36. **Avoid.** No scene where he is only a rich man or only the comic factory boss. No scene where he explains Kenji's secrets. No sudden gratitude speeches.

## Act I facts (author rulings, 3 November 2026)

- **Recruited in July 2052** (A2.3, his POV) by Kenji in a private hospital room, with Vance and four fighters waiting in the corridor. Kenji restores his prime years in exchange for Oyama: his death is staged, he returns as Anthony Strauss, and he runs Oyama for Kenji (author ruling, 5 November 2026). Oyama builds what Kenji designs under no name but Tallis Bay. **Oyama first builds industrial versions of Kenji's Phased Disassembler Array**, the top-of-the-line version of what Kenji built crudely himself, which vaporize anything, break it down to base components, and sort the elements by atomic mass, so the organization can buy scrap across the country and never run short of materials (author ruling, 4 November 2026). **Oyama builds the Primitive Med-Pods** (Kenji builds none), Level 1 robots (and later Levels 2 and 3), Level 1 powered suits, Tokamak reactors, Level 1 anti-gravity, and Gundarium Alpha once orbital infrastructure is complete.
- **The security-droid design.** Kenji gives him the design for Oyama's own use as security droids, not for sale, and supplies the data crystals and Level 2 Dumb AI himself. Byron keeps the drawings in his own safe.
- **First target of Cerberus.** In January 2058, when Oyama's Primitive pods go on sale, a law firm acting for a buyer no one can name offers to buy Oyama's technology patents. He refuses and calls Kenji. A second offer follows in April with a forged safety audit, and a third in October. He refuses all three.
- **Later.** He becomes one of the Imperium's top administrators (title open). Oyama belongs to Kenji, and Byron runs it for him.
