# Eleanor Whitcombe: Character Profile

Anything I invented to fill a gap is marked **(decision)** and is yours to overrule. The name, age, health, and committee are placeholders. The profile is the base for how Whitcombe acts in every scene. Plain language throughout (General Writing Rules, rule 52).

## Part A. Who she is

1. **Name.** Senator Eleanor Whitcombe. Staff call her "Senator." Her colleagues call her "Ellie" and she dislikes it. Kenji calls her "Senator" and then "Eleanor." **(decision)**
2. **Birth year.** 1993, so she is sixty in February 2053 and seventy-one in 2064 (A4.3). **(decision)** Her age matters because she is hiding a failing heart and cannot be seen to be weak. After treatment she lives a long time, and later becomes one of the Imperium's top administrators (title open).
3. **Region of origin.** A small Gulf Coast town in Louisiana, where her father was a county clerk and her mother ran a church kitchen. **(decision)**
4. **Languages.** English with a soft Louisiana accent that comes back when she is tired. She uses plain words and short sentences in public, and long careful ones in a private room.
5. **Trade.** Best: legislator and committee chair, someone who knows how a bill moves and who can stop it. Second-best: county clerk, her father's job. **(decision)**
6. **Money and standing.** She is a senator who chairs the committee that licenses medical devices. She is comfortable and owns no company. People owe her favors in every ministry she has ever dealt with, and she never mentions it.
7. **Appearance.**
   - A tall, thin woman with silver hair worn up and reading glasses pushed up on her head.
   - A navy suit and a small gold pin of a ship's wheel, her father's.
   - She stands with one hand on the back of a chair, as if she were thinking, and she is really resting.

## Part B. What drives her

8. **Small want.** Her answer given before her committee votes on Thursday.
9. **Large want.** To lead people who need her and can do something with her help. Underneath that, to be well enough to do it for another decade. She would not say either aloud.
10. **Fear.** Being seen as weak. If it is known that her heart is failing, she loses her chair and her power, and everyone she has helped loses a friend.
11. **Wound.** At thirty-six she lost an election by two hundred votes because her doctor's diagnosis leaked. **(decision)** She learned that people forgive a lie sooner than a weakness.
12. **Pride.** Reading a room. When someone tells her she misjudged it, she does not argue. She asks them what they saw, and then she says whether they were right.

## Part C. How she sees the world

13. **Habit of attention.** Who looks at whom before they speak. She walks into a room and sees which person glances at an aide, which looks at the door, and which looks at the person who is going to answer for them.
14. **What she misreads.** Plain honesty.
    - She assumes everyone is watching someone else for permission, and she is slow to believe a person who says what they think.
    - She assumes that a gift is a trade.
    - She underrates how much of her caution is fear of being found out.
15. **View of the age.** She trusts a cure she has felt in her own chest. She does not trust a cure with no owner, and she asks quietly who is behind it. **(decision)**
16. **Humor.** Dry and political. "In my experience, a man who says he'll be brief has already decided how long." She never jokes about someone's health.

## Part D. How she behaves

17. **Speech.** Calm, formal, and warm. She asks "Who spoke first?" when she wants to know who is really deciding.
    - Calm: "I'd like to know who spoke first when the question came up, and who they were looking at when they answered. Then I'll know who I'm really talking to."
    - Frustrated: "Senator Doyle told me on Monday that the committee would take no position, and now he's circulating a draft. Either he changed his mind, or someone changed it for him, and I'd like to know which."
    - Lying or avoiding: "I'm perfectly well, thank you. I've simply been up late with the budget."
18. **Body tells.** She pushes her reading glasses up onto her head. When it is bad she presses two fingers to her chest as if she were straightening the pin.
19. **Stress response.** She takes the chair. When a witness walked out of a hearing, she called a recess, found him in the hall, and asked him what he needed. **(decision)** She does not let a room see her slow down.
20. **Honesty.** 3 out of 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie). She keeps her health and her votes private, and she does not lie about a fact that someone else will check.
21. **Authority.** She respects people who can deliver, and she expects the same of herself. She treats her staff as colleagues, and she remembers the name of every clerk.

## Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. **Who she protects.** In order: the people in her factions, her committee, and Kenji's organization.
23. **Who she distrusts.** Offers with no name behind them. Aides who answer for their bosses. Anyone who wants her to vote before she has seen who is asking.
24. **Who or what she owes.** Kenji, for her heart. Marchetti, who brought her the invitation.
25. **Loyalty under pressure.** Her office against the organization. In October 2061 she uses her faction to stop the other clinics from closing and to force Galveston to reopen, though that costs her favors she has saved for years. **(decision)**
26. **Line and price.** She will never use the faction to hurt a patient. What could make her is a threat to the faction itself, and then she says so in front of the committee.
27. **Secret.** She has not told her family about her heart. **(decision)** She still has not told them when she is seventy-one.

## Part F. How she changes

28. **Adaptability.** In any life she would still watch who looks at whom, keep her health private, and wear her father's pin.
29. **What could break her.** Her faction being used to hurt the people it was meant to protect.
30. **What could redeem her.** She will stay after a hearing to sit with a frightened witness and ask what they need.

## Part G. Rules for the Author

31. **Rule 1:** If a room goes quiet, then she watches who looks at whom before she speaks.
32. **Rule 2:** If someone she is responsible for is threatened, then she calls a vote or a recess and buys time.
33. **Rule 3:** If she is asked about her health, then she changes the subject to the work.

## Part H. Optional

34. **Object.** Her father's ship's-wheel pin. **(decision)**
35. **Memory.** The election night she lost by two hundred votes, and the faces of the people who would not look at her.
36. **Avoid.** No scene where she is only a politician who is hiding something. No speeches. No sudden warmth toward Kenji that earlier scenes have not built.
