# Tom Shepard: Character Profile

Anything I invented to fill a gap is marked **(decision)** and is yours to overrule. The profile is the base for how Tom acts in every scene.

## Part A. Who he is

1. **Name.** Tom Shepard. Everyone calls him "Tom." Ruth calls him "Tommy" when she is teasing or worried. Hannah is "Darling" to him, and he says it in front of her crew without caring. She calls him "Dad." Before the wedding Kenji calls him "Sir," and after it he calls him "Dad." Tom calls Kenji "son" in private. He dislikes being called "Pops" by younger workers, and corrects them once. **(decision)**
2. **Birth year.** 2001. He is fifty-two when he is dying of pancreatic cancer and gets cured, and he lives to 116 on the Level 2 treatment Kenji gives the found family, because only Level 2 can extend life and the public clinics cannot. **(decision)** His age matters because he was within weeks of dying, and every year after is one he did not expect.
3. **Region of origin.** Galveston, Texas, in a family that has worked the same shipyards for three generations. The street smelled of salt, diesel, and welding smoke, and everyone knew whose father could fix what.
4. **Languages.** English with a thick Gulf Coast accent that he never tried to lose. Working Spanish from forty years of crews. He swears in both when he is angry, and in neither when a child is in the room.
5. **Trade.** Best: engineer. He never finished a degree and learned on the job **(decision)**, but he has the mind of a first-rate systems engineer. He sees how a machine fits together and why it fails, and he can follow advanced designs that stump trained people. He worked his way up as a shipyard foreman, and he fights to be chief engineer of the test ship. Second-best: facilities and maintenance chief, the job he takes at the clinics, keeping a building alive through the night.
6. **Money and standing.** At fifty-two he has debt from his illness and almost no savings. Later he has a steady wage and no reason to spend it. He owns his own tools and little else. Crews who worked under him owe him jobs and loyalty, and he never calls it in.
7. **Appearance.**
   - Big, thick-fingered hands covered in old burn scars, and a stoop in the shoulders that comes from forty years of leaning over work.
   - A faded cap, reading glasses on a cord around his neck, and steel-toed boots he wears even to dinner. A stub of pencil sits behind one ear, and a small notebook of sketches is always in his shirt pocket.
   - He stands with his hands on his hips and looks at the thing in front of him before he speaks.

## Part B. What drives him

8. **Small want.** To get the building through the night: the pipe fixed, the generator running, the shift crew home safe.
9. **Large want.** To be chief engineer of the first test ship and see it fly, and to see his daughter out of danger. He would not say the second aloud.
10. **Fear.** His daughter being hurt, and being useless or kept away from the work. He will not run a building or a crew he has not checked himself.
11. **Wound.** Two. As a young foreman he lost a welder on his crew when a gantry cable failed, and he has checked every cable twice since then. The welder was Ray Iglesias, and Tom and Ruth took in his orphaned son Danny. **(decision)** Then he was dying, and watching his wife wait. The first taught him that every shortcut is paid for by someone else. The second taught him that nobody owns tomorrow.
12. **Pride.** Understanding how a machine works from the inside and building it right. He can tell a bad weld by the sound, and he can read a drawing and see where it will fail. When someone says he is wrong about a job, he does not argue. He asks to see it, checks it with his own hands, and fixes it before he says a word.

## Part C. How he sees the world

13. **Habit of attention.** Hands and tools. He walks into a room and sees what each person's hands are doing, which tool is missing from the bench, who holds a wrench by the wrong end, and whose hands are too clean for the job they claim to do.
14. **What he misreads.** People who talk well.
    - He trusts people who work with their hands and is slow to suspect them. He assumes anyone can understand a machine if they take the time, and he is impatient with people who will not try.
    - He reads a quiet man's silence as shyness or humility, when it is often a secret.
    - He does not notice money moving. An attack through paper or contracts looks to him like office noise.
15. **View of the age.** He is practical and grateful. The cure gave him back his life, so he uses the clinics and trusts the machines. He is fascinated by them. He studies each one the way other men study a puzzle, wanting to know how it works and not only that it works. He watches the robots work and notices they never tire and never hurry, and he is not sure whether that is good for the men beside them.
16. **Humor.** Dry and shipyard-plain, usually about the job or the weather. "A leaking boat doesn't care who's embarrassed, so let's fix it first and be embarrassed later." He never jokes about the dead.

## Part D. How he behaves

17. **Speech.** Slow, warm, and concrete. He answers in a few sentences, starting with the thing in front of him, and he does not rush to fill a silence. His verbal habit is "Show me," said to anyone who describes a problem without pointing at it.
    - Calm: "Show me where it's leaking. I don't want to guess from here, and I don't want you guessing either. Once I've seen it, we'll know what we're working with."
    - Frustrated: "No, that's not the right wrench, and you know it isn't. You've got four better ones on the bench and you picked the one that was closest. Go back and get the right one, and don't tell me you're in a hurry."
    - Lying or avoiding: "Oh, it's just the heat getting to me. I've been on my feet since four this morning. Let's go in and eat."
18. **Body tell****s****.** He turns a tool over in his hands, a wrench or a screwdriver, without looking at it. When it is bad he grips the nearest tool and will not put it down, even to shake someone's hand.
19. **Stress response.** He takes the physical job. He shuts the valve, moves the people, and carries the load. On the night of the 2059 bomb he wanted to stay behind to shut off the oxygen line, and his daughter ordered him out. He obeyed, and it is the only order of hers he never argued with. **(decision)**
20. **Honesty.** 4 out of 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie). He almost never lies, and he is bad at it. He keeps things from Ruth by not saying them, and she knows it. Danny, whom he has not seen in years, asked Kenji to save him. He took the knowledge upload but not the conditioning, so he can speak freely, and he chooses to stay quiet about what he sees.
21. **Authority.** He respects people who know the work and has no patience for people who don't. With a crew he is gruff, fair, and the first one on the dangerous job. With those above him he says what he thinks, once, and then does the job. He asks Kenji straight out to make him chief engineer of the test ship, and argues for it with reasons: no one else will care about the engines as much as he does. He calls Kenji "son" in private, and means it.

## Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. **Who he protects.** In order: Ruth, Hannah and Danny (his adopted son), Kenji, and the crew he foremans. He would put the crew above himself without being asked.
23. **Who he distrusts.** People who talk about work but never touch it. Men with sidearms who have never worked a bench. And helpful strangers who turn up in the same corridor three nights in a row.
24. **Who or what he owes.** Kenji, for the cure and the second life. The welder he lost. His crews, for putting up with a foreman who checked everything twice.
25. **Loyalty under pressure.** Kenji's silence against his daughter's need for the truth. He gave his word to Kenji, so he keeps what he knows from Hannah and tells Ruth only what he has to. The cost is that Hannah sometimes looks at him and sees a man who is holding something back.
26. **Line and price.** He will never run a building or a crew he knows is unsafe. What could make him break it is a clinic closing and people going untreated, and then he runs it with every precaution he can build and watches it all night.
27. **Secret.** From the knowledge he took in and the machines he has worked on, he knows these designs are too advanced for any laboratory on Earth. He suspects Kenji is the person behind them and built much of it himself. He has never asked, and he has told no one.

## Part F. How he changes

28. **Adaptability.** In any life he would still take things apart to see how they work, check the weld twice, notice what people's hands are doing, say "Show me," and call a younger man "son."
29. **What could break him.** Losing Hannah, or finding that a crew he built was used to hurt people. Or finding out that what he kept quiet about was what hurt his family.
30. **What could redeem him.** He teaches every apprentice who comes to him, including the ones sent to watch him. He stays late to fix what is not his job.

## Part G. Rules for the Author

31. **Rule 1:** If he is not sure something is safe or does not understand how it works, then he takes it apart on paper first, checks it himself with his own hands, and lets no one rely on it until he understands it.
32. **Rule 2:** If someone he trusts hides something from him, then he watches what their hands do and says nothing until he is sure.
33. **Rule 3:** If someone he loves is hurt or in danger, then he takes the physical job nearest to them and does it until it is finished.

## Part H. Optional

34. **Object.** His father's adjustable wrench, with the initials scratched into the handle, kept in his back pocket. **(decision)**
35. **Memory.** The morning after the cure, standing barefoot on the clinic floor, his hands no longer hurting, and asking for breakfast.
36. **Avoid.** No scene where he is only comic relief or only the wise old man. No scene where he works out the secrets for himself. No scene where he gives a long speech.

## Rules this profile follows

- Dialogue never runs in single-sentence replies. He answers in several sentences, starting with the thing in front of him.
- He knows only what he has seen and been told. He suspects Kenji is behind the technology, but he does not know where it came from or why it exists.
- He has the knowledge upload but not the conditioning. He is free to speak and disagree, and he stays by choice.
