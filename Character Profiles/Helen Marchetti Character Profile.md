# Helen Marchetti: Character Profile

Anything I invented to fill a gap is marked **(decision)** and is yours to overrule. The name, age, and history are placeholders. The profile is the base for how Marchetti acts in every scene. Plain language throughout (General Writing Rules, rule 52).

## Part A. Who she is

1. **Name.** Helen Marchetti. Her partners call her "Helen." Kenji calls her "Ms. Marchetti" at first, then "Helen." Clients call her "Counselor," which she dislikes. **(decision)**
2. **Birth year.** 1999, so she is fifty-two in 2051, when her kidneys are failing. **(decision)** Her age matters because she has built a career on knowing what a contract can survive, and she now has to trust one man with her own life. After treatment she lives a long time, and later becomes one of the Imperium's top administrators (title open).
3. **Region of origin.** Pasadena, Texas, a refinery town east of Houston, where her father ran a small insurance office and her mother kept the books. **(decision)**
4. **Languages.** English with a Gulf Coast accent that goes flat in court. Fluent Spanish from her mother's side. She speaks in complete sentences and chooses each word as if it will be read back to her.
5. **Trade.** Best: foundation and corporate lawyer, someone who builds structures that outlast the people who set them up. Second-best: arbitrator. **(decision)**
6. **Money and standing.** She is a partner in a medium-sized Houston firm and comfortable. She owns her books and her pens and little else. After 2051 she is the trustee of Tallis Bay, so she is the one person who can say no to Kenji.
7. **Appearance.**
   - A small, upright woman with short gray hair and heavy dark-framed glasses.
   - A black fountain pen she caps and uncaps while she listens.
   - A tailored dark suit and low heels, always, even in a hospital room.

## Part B. What drives her

8. **Small want.** The foundation filed before the quarter closes, so the first gift can be accepted tax-free.
9. **Large want.** To draw up one structure that nobody can break, and to see it still standing. Underneath that, to be needed for something other than saying no. She would not say either aloud.
10. **Fear.** A gap in a contract that someone else finds first. She will not sign anything she has not read to the end.
11. **Wound.** Early in her career a client's company failed because she missed one clause in a sale. **(decision)** She learned that the gap is always in the line you read last.
12. **Pride.** Drafting. When someone tells her a clause is wrong, she does not argue. She asks them to point to the sentence, reads it aloud, and either fixes it or shows them where it holds.

## Part C. How she sees the world

13. **Habit of attention.** Who signs, and who can be sued. She walks into a room and sees who has the pen, who has the authority to hold it, and who will be left holding the blame.
14. **What she misreads.** Gratitude and loyalty.
    - She assumes people act from what the paper makes them do, and she is slow to see that some people stay because they want to.
    - She assumes a person who gives her everything must be hiding something.
    - She underrates how much of her caution is fear of being wrong.
15. **View of the age.** She trusts a good contract more than a good cure, and she is surprised that she trusts both. She does not ask where the designs come from, because her client told her not to and she believes him. **(decision)**
16. **Humor.** Dry and legal. "I'd call that an oral agreement, but that would imply it was ever spoken." She never jokes about a client's illness.

## Part D. How she behaves

17. **Speech.** Careful, complete, and slow. She finishes every sentence, and she asks "Who signs that?" before she agrees to anything.
    - Calm: "Before I answer, I want to see the clause. If the foundation owns the design, then the foundation has to be able to sell it, and I need to know who decides that. Who signs?"
    - Frustrated: "No. You wrote 'may' where it has to say 'shall,' and a court will read that as a choice. Change the word and send it back to me tonight."
    - Lying or avoiding: "I'm sure it's fine. I haven't read that part closely, and I'd rather not guess."
18. **Body tells.** She caps and uncaps her pen. When it is bad she sets the pen down and puts both hands on the page.
19. **Stress response.** She goes slow and precise. When a client was arrested in front of her, she asked for the warrant and read every line before she spoke. **(decision)**
20. **Honesty.** 4 out of 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie). She does not say false things, but she chooses her words so that she is never asked a question she cannot answer.
21. **Authority.** She respects the person who can be held to account and has no patience for someone who gives orders and signs nothing. She treats the people under her as colleagues and expects them to read the whole page.

## Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. **Who she protects.** In order: the foundation (Tallis Bay), the people on its terms, and Kenji.
23. **Who she distrusts.** Anyone who will not put a name on a page. Lawyers who will not say who their client is. Deals that are urgent for only one side.
24. **Who or what she owes.** Kenji, for her kidneys and her years. Ortega, her partner, who was dying the same autumn.
25. **Loyalty under pressure.** The foundation against Kenji. In October 2051 he makes her trustee, which means she can stop him, and she keeps that duty even when he wants something fast. **(decision)** The cost is that he sometimes has to ask her instead of telling her.
26. **Line and price.** She will never sign a paper she has not read to the end. What could make her is a patient dying in the next room, and then she signs with a note saying exactly what she did not read.
27. **Secret.** She keeps an undated resignation letter in her desk, in case she ever has to stop Kenji and walk away. **(decision)** She has never used it.

## Part F. How she changes

28. **Adaptability.** In any life she would still read to the end, ask who signs, and carry a black pen.
29. **What could break her.** A gap in a structure she built, found by someone who uses it against the people she protects.
30. **What could redeem her.** She reads the contract aloud to anyone frightened of it, and she stays until they understand every line.

## Part G. Rules for the Author

31. **Rule 1:** If she is asked to agree to something, then she asks who signs and what happens if that person disappears.
32. **Rule 2:** If someone she is responsible for is threatened, then she goes to the paper first and finds the one clause that protects them.
33. **Rule 3:** If she is thanked, then she says what the next filing is and picks up the pen.

## Part H. Optional

34. **Object.** The black fountain pen, a gift from her father when she passed the bar. **(decision)**
35. **Memory.** Her father's office in Pasadena, with every policy in a drawer, and the day a client lost a house because one line was missing.
36. **Avoid.** No scene where she is only the lawyer who says no. No long legal explanations. No sudden affection for Kenji that earlier scenes have not built.
