# Teodor Ashby: Character Profile

Anything I invented to fill a gap is marked **(decision)** and is yours to overrule. The profile is the base for how Ashby acts in every scene.

## Part A. Who he is

1. **Name.** Teodor Ashby. Everyone calls him "Ashby." Vance calls him "Ashby" too, and "kid" when he is worried. His old squad called him "Sparks," and he no longer answers to it, because the only men who used it are dead. **(decision)** Kenji calls him "Teodor."
2. **Birth year.** 2030. He is nineteen when the squad is destroyed in the trenches, thirty-three when he oversees the droid test range, and thirty-five when his crews find the cavern on Mars. His age matters because he was the youngest in the squad and the only technician to survive it.
3. **Region of origin.** Duluth, Minnesota, a cold port town of ore ships, radio towers, and long winters. He grew up in a house full of repaired radios and a grandmother who spoke Polish when she was angry. **(decision)**
4. **Languages.** English, with a flat Minnesota accent. Some Polish from his grandmother, mostly swearing and endearments. He talks quietly and precisely, and he repeats a number back to make sure he has it right.
5. **Trade.** Best: field technician and systems diagnostician, someone who keeps complex machines running and knows why they fail. Second-best: radio and electronics repairman, the work he did as a teenager.
6. **Money and standing.** After the trenches he has a medical discharge, a disability pension, and nothing else. Later the network pays him well and he spends almost none of it. He sends a share every month to the families of the men who died. He owns his tools, and his tools are all he owns.
7. **Appearance.**
   - Lean and wiry, not tall, with close-cropped dark hair and pale eyes that look slightly to one side when he is listening.
   - An old mechanic's stethoscope around his neck, which he uses on machines.
   - A faint hitch in his left leg that comes back in cold weather, and a scar on the same knee.

## Part B. What drives him

8. **Small want.** The range log signed before the crews rotate.
9. **Large want.** To make sure no machine fails silently again. Underneath that, to have the names of the squad said aloud by someone who knew them. He would not say the second aloud.
10. **Fear.** Silence. A machine too quiet, a radio with nothing on it, a room where nothing is wrong. He will not sign off on anything he has not listened to himself.
11. **Wound.** In the trenches, a few hours before the squad was ambushed on bad intelligence, he heard a drift in the timing of the relay radio and decided it was nothing. **(decision)** He did not report it. He has never told anyone. The squad was destroyed, and he was one of the few survivors. He learned that a fault you hear and do not speak of belongs to you.
12. **Pride.** Diagnosis. He can find what is wrong with a machine by its sound before any instrument shows it. When someone tells him he is wrong, he does not argue. He plays the recording back and lets them hear it.

## Part C. How he sees the world

13. **Habit of attention.** What machines are doing wrong. He walks into a room and hears the hum that is a half tone off, the fan that is slightly late, the click that does not belong. He notices when something is too quiet.
14. **What he misreads.** Perfection, and people.
    - He distrusts a machine that never complains, and assumes it is hiding something.
    - He expects people to show a fault before they fail, and is thrown when someone fails without warning.
    - He reads silence as danger, and cannot tell calm from concealment.
15. **View of the age.** He loves the machines and is uneasy about them. The new drive worries him more than any broken one, because it makes no noise and gives him nothing to catch. He suspects the people who made it understand something he does not.
16. **Humor.** Dry and deadpan, usually about machines. "If it's loud, I'm employed. If it's quiet, I'm worried." He does not joke about the squad.

## Part D. How he behaves

17. **Speech.** Quiet, precise, and complete. He answers in a few sentences, giving the observation first and the explanation second. His verbal habit is "Listen to that," said before he explains anything he can hear.
    - Calm: "Listen to that. There's a rattle every fourth beat, under the main hum. It isn't loud enough to matter today, but it will be in a week. I'd rather we look at it now."
    - Frustrated: "No, I won't sign that. I've been on the range since six, and the second unit has been wrong all morning. It isn't failing yet, which is the problem. If you want my name on the log, give me another hour."
    - Lying or avoiding: "No, I didn't hear anything. It was a long shift, that's all. I'd just like to finish the log and go home."
18. **Body tell****s****.** He tilts his head and goes very still, listening, and touches the stethoscope at his neck. When it is bad he takes it off and holds it in both hands.
19. **Stress response.** He goes silent and still for a few seconds, listening, and then moves fast. In the trenches he stood frozen for two seconds and then dragged the wounded to cover. He has never forgiven himself for the two seconds.
20. **Honesty.** 4 out of 5 (1 lies easily, 5 cannot lie). He almost never lies. He is silent when he is not sure, and he has paid for that silence once. He has never told anyone that he heard the fault before the ambush.
21. **Authority.** He respects Vance without question, and respects anyone who listens. He has no patience for rank without competence. Since the trenches he reports every fault he hears, in writing, to whoever is above him, even when they do not want it.

## Part E. Loyalties and limits

22. **Who he protects.** In order: Vance, the crews who run the machines he keeps working, and the families of the dead squad. Later, the man Vance guards, and then the people who stand with them.
23. **Who he distrusts.** Machines that never complain. People who say "it's fine" without a log. And officers who give orders from far away.
24. **Who or what he owes.** Vance, who found him after the trenches and gave him work. Kenji, for the treatment that fixed his knee and his lungs. The squad.
25. **Loyalty under pressure.** Justice for the squad against his loyalty to Vance. The army wanted him to testify against the officer who sent them in. He refused, and joined Vance instead. The cost was that the officer was never held to account, and Ashby lost his pension. **(decision)**
26. **Line and price.** He will never sign off on a machine he has not listened to. What could make him break it is a deadline with lives behind it, and then he signs with a written note saying exactly what he does not know.
27. **Secret.** He heard the fault before the ambush and said nothing. He has never told anyone, including Vance.

## Part F. How he changes

28. **Adaptability.** In any life he would still listen first, sign only what he trusts, and carry the names of the squad.
29. **What could break him.** A machine he approved failing and killing people. Or learning that the drive he trusts too easily has been hiding something all along.
30. **What could redeem him.** He teaches others to listen to machines and to speak up. He reads the squad's names aloud on the day they died. He will stay at the range at three in the morning to hear a machine out for someone he does not know.

## Part G. Rules for the Author

31. **Rule 1:** If a machine is running perfectly and quietly, then he distrusts it and checks it harder than a broken one.
32. **Rule 2:** If he hears something wrong, then he reports it at once, in writing, even when no one wants to know.
33. **Rule 3:** If someone he trusts tells him not to worry, then he says "all right" and goes on listening anyway.

## Part H. Optional

34. **Object.** An old mechanic's stethoscope, the earpieces taped where they cracked. **(decision)**
35. **Memory.** Two. The first is the relay radio in the trenches, and the small drift in its timing that he heard and ignored. The second is the cavern on Mars, where his crews' lamps found the first body and every machine around him made no sound.
36. **Avoid.** No scene where he is only the nerd or only the damaged veteran. No long technical explanations of how the drive works. No jokes about the dead squad.

## Part I. Later

In the years after the early network he becomes part of Kenji's personal guard squad, the Custodians, and receives the full enhancement and loyalty conditioning that go with it. He is not conditioned when he is recruited. He joins Vance by choice.

## Rules this profile follows

- Dialogue never runs in single-sentence replies. He answers in several sentences, observation first and explanation second.
- He knows only what he has seen and heard. He does not know who is behind the network or why it exists.
- He listens to machines. His attention is sound, timing, and silence, and he counts nothing.
